Digital NSW publishes [Understanding responsibilities in AI practices](/policies/nsw-responsible-ai-guidance), guidance "for understanding roles and responsibilities to ensure responsible artificial intelligence (AI) practices" in NSW Government agencies ([Digital NSW](https://www.digital.nsw.gov.au/policy/artificial-intelligence/nsw-artificial-intelligence-assessment-framework/responsibilities)). The register holds it as active NSW guidance, dated to December 2024 at month precision ([record](/policies/nsw-responsible-ai-guidance)).

## What it says

The guidance starts from the position that "Responsible AI is a collective responsibility, encompassing all levels within an agency, from executives to end users" ([Digital NSW](https://www.digital.nsw.gov.au/policy/artificial-intelligence/nsw-artificial-intelligence-assessment-framework/responsibilities)). It says it is based on ISO/IEC standards and aligned with the [NSW AI Assessment Framework](/policies/nsw-artificial-intelligence-assessment-framework), and that executives are "ultimately accountable" while responsible use involves everyone in an agency, "not just technical or product teams" ([Digital NSW](https://www.digital.nsw.gov.au/policy/artificial-intelligence/nsw-artificial-intelligence-assessment-framework/responsibilities)).

It defines five roles: the executive level, such as CEOs, CFOs, CIOs, CDOs and department heads; the management level, with "a strong focus on governance, assurance, cybersecurity, legal, record keeping, ethics, policy, and risk management"; product owners; users; and everyone ([Digital NSW](https://www.digital.nsw.gov.au/policy/artificial-intelligence/nsw-artificial-intelligence-assessment-framework/responsibilities)). It then allocates actions in RACI matrices (accountable, responsible, consulted, informed) under five objectives: fostering a responsible AI culture, ensuring accountability and transparency, allocating resources, ensuring compliance and risk management, and establishing oversight mechanisms ([Digital NSW](https://www.digital.nsw.gov.au/policy/artificial-intelligence/nsw-artificial-intelligence-assessment-framework/responsibilities)).

Among the actions it allocates ([Digital NSW](https://www.digital.nsw.gov.au/policy/artificial-intelligence/nsw-artificial-intelligence-assessment-framework/responsibilities)):

- **Transparency:** publishing "regular transparency reports for high-risk or customer-facing AI systems", covering use cases, performance, governance practices and any incidents or interventions, with the executive level accountable;
- **Accountability:** documenting, for each AI solution, accountabilities for managing risks, ensuring continuity, enabling appeals and providing evidence for decisions and actions, and keeping records of risk decisions such as the results of applying the assessment framework;
- **Oversight:** designating an Accountable Official to oversee the NSW AI Operational Policy, creating a multidisciplinary AI advisory board or committee, and arranging regular independent reviews of AI governance and assurance functions;
- **Risk:** presenting high-risk AI projects and solutions to the AI Review Committee, giving AI solutions of medium or higher risk incident response plans with "tested, monitored, and communicated appeal processes that include human intervention", and making sure high-risk solutions can explain their outputs and trace decisions back to their source data and logic; and
- **Everyone:** reporting a solution that "may influence decisions or actions that could be unethical, illegal, or unsafe", with every staff member accountable and responsible for doing so.

The guidance asks staff to find the role that fits them, consider how the responsibilities fit their team's workflows and, if they manage others, whether any belong in performance plans ([Digital NSW](https://www.digital.nsw.gov.au/policy/artificial-intelligence/nsw-artificial-intelligence-assessment-framework/responsibilities)). It says agencies "should start by ensuring a Governance and Assurance function has clear accountability for overseeing responsible AI use" ([Digital NSW](https://www.digital.nsw.gov.au/policy/artificial-intelligence/nsw-artificial-intelligence-assessment-framework/responsibilities)).

## Who it affects

The guidance is written for NSW Government agencies and public servants, who "can use" it and are "encouraged" to build its responsibilities into performance plans, AI awareness training and internal governance and assurance processes ([Digital NSW](https://www.digital.nsw.gov.au/policy/artificial-intelligence/nsw-artificial-intelligence-assessment-framework/responsibilities)).

It sits under the [NSW AI Operational Policy](/policies/nsw-ai-operational-policy), which sets "mandatory agency requirements" for AI use in the NSW Government ([NSW Government](https://www.digital.nsw.gov.au/policy/artificial-intelligence/ai-strategy-and-operational-policy/nsw-ai-operational-policy)). Under that policy, agencies must appoint an accountable official, register all AI use cases in the AI Assessment Framework Platform, apply the framework where registration determines an assessment is required, and refer high-risk and critical-risk use cases to the AI Review Committee ([NSW Government](https://www.digital.nsw.gov.au/policy/artificial-intelligence/ai-strategy-and-operational-policy/nsw-ai-operational-policy)). Public servants who use AI in their work must complete AI literacy and policy training and report AI incidents, safety issues or non-compliance ([NSW Government](https://www.digital.nsw.gov.au/policy/artificial-intelligence/ai-strategy-and-operational-policy/nsw-ai-operational-policy)). The NSW Government says compliance is mandated by Circular DCS-2026-02 and that the framework's platform has been mandatory for registering and assessing AI use cases since 30 September 2026 ([NSW Government](https://www.digital.nsw.gov.au/policy/artificial-intelligence/ai-governance-assurance-and-frameworks/nsw-ai-assessment-framework)).

## Analysis

In Policai's analysis, the operational policy says what an agency must do, and this guidance says who inside the agency does it. The policy requires an accountable official; the guidance goes further and names the product owner who must keep the evidence and the user who must raise the alarm.

In Policai's analysis, the most consequential line is the transparency report for high-risk or customer-facing systems. It goes further than registering a use case internally, because it would put performance and incident information in front of the public. The guidance frames it as an allocated action rather than a mandatory requirement, so whether NSW agencies actually publish such reports is a question the guidance raises rather than settles.

In Policai's analysis, the guidance and the policy now use slightly different thresholds: the guidance speaks of high-risk AI projects going to the AI Review Committee, while the operational policy refers to high-risk and critical-risk use cases. Agencies building the RACI into their own processes should work from the policy's wording, since the policy is the instrument that binds.
