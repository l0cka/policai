The NSW Government has rewritten [Identifying AI](/policies/nsw-identifying-ai-guidance),
its guidance for public servants who need to work out whether a digital
solution uses artificial intelligence. The
[current page](https://www.digital.nsw.gov.au/policy/artificial-intelligence/nsw-artificial-intelligence-assessment-framework/identifying-ai)
states that "From 30 September 2026, the AIAF Platform is the authoritative
channel for AI use case registration and assessment across NSW Government",
and that "All agencies must register their AI use cases." Policai published
the updated record on 16 October 2026.

## What it says

The guidance is for public servants involved in "the lifecycle of digital
solutions, from design and sourcing to procurement, selection and
management". It describes itself as general guidance only, and "not an
exhaustive source for determining whether AI is present or whether an AIAF
assessment is required"
([Identifying AI](https://www.digital.nsw.gov.au/policy/artificial-intelligence/nsw-artificial-intelligence-assessment-framework/identifying-ai)).

It offers two tools. The first is a set of five contrasts between AI and
conventional software, such as probabilistic against deterministic behaviour.
The second is a questionnaire. A system is unlikely to use AI if the answer is
yes to all eight "non-AI" questions. It is likely to use AI if the answer is
yes to any one of seven "AI" questions, which include whether the system can
"analyse, interpret or generate content from complex formats such as text,
images, audio or documents".

The outcome is now one of two actions. If a solution uses or may use AI, the
agency registers the use case in the AIAF Platform, and "The registration
process will determine whether an AIAF assessment is required." If it does
not, the agency documents that decision under its own governance and
records-management requirements, and reviews it if the solution's features
or use change.

An agency that cannot reach the platform because its SectorLink onboarding is
incomplete can request a controlled offline assessment from NSW GovAI. The
[NSW AI Assessment Framework page](https://www.digital.nsw.gov.au/policy/artificial-intelligence/ai-governance-assurance-and-frameworks/nsw-ai-assessment-framework)
states that this pathway "is not a general alternative to the platform and
cannot be used by preference".

## What changed

The version of this page that Policai verified on 19 July 2026 left agencies
to decide whether the AIAF applied. It suggested applying the AIAF wherever
AI use was possible, and required agencies to document any decision not to
apply it. The current page moves that
decision to the platform: the agency registers the use case, and the
registration process decides whether an assessment is needed.

The page now carries no date. The register keeps December 2024, at month
precision, as the guidance's publication date. That date came from the
filename of the original downloadable guidance, which the page no longer
links
([Identifying AI](/policies/nsw-identifying-ai-guidance)).

## Who it affects

The guidance applies to NSW Government agencies and the public servants who
design, source, buy and manage their digital solutions. The
[NSW AI Operational Policy](/policies/nsw-ai-operational-policy) requires
agencies to "Ensure that all AI use cases are registered in the AI Assessment
Framework Platform" and to apply the
[AI Assessment Framework](/policies/nsw-artificial-intelligence-assessment-framework)
"where the registration process determines an assessment is required". The
[policy page](https://www.digital.nsw.gov.au/policy/artificial-intelligence/ai-strategy-and-operational-policy/nsw-ai-operational-policy)
states that Circular DCS-2026-02 mandates compliance, and refers readers to
the circular for the entities it covers.

Use cases assessed as high or critical risk must go to the AI Secretariat for
review by the NSW AI Review Committee, according to the
[AIAF page](https://www.digital.nsw.gov.au/policy/artificial-intelligence/ai-governance-assurance-and-frameworks/nsw-ai-assessment-framework).

## Analysis

In Policai's analysis, the practical change is who decides whether an
assessment is needed. Under the July version, the agency made that call and
recorded it. Now the agency registers and the platform decides, so every
use case that may involve AI becomes visible across government. The
guidance says "Where there is a possibility that a system uses AI,
register the use case", and a single "yes" to the AI questions points to
registration. On that test, many everyday products with built-in
transcription, summarisation or chat features would be registered, even
where the agency treats the AI feature as minor.

Two official pages describe the transition differently. The AIAF page states
that agencies "must use the AIAF Platform for all new AI use case
registrations and assessments" from 30 September 2026. The
[Operational Policy page](https://www.digital.nsw.gov.au/policy/artificial-intelligence/ai-strategy-and-operational-policy/nsw-ai-operational-policy)
still states that "agencies must continue to apply the existing AI Assessment
Framework Excel-based assessment process to all AI use cases until the
platform is fully adopted". Neither page says how use cases already assessed
in the Excel process move to the platform. Agencies with existing
assessments may want to confirm the position with NSW GovAI.

The missing page date matters for readers who rely on the guidance. Without a
version or date, a reader cannot tell from the page alone which text applied
on a given day. Policai's record shows when it last verified the page against
its source.
