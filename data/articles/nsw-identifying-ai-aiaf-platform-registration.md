NSW agencies must register any digital solution that uses, or may use, AI in the AIAF Platform. The NSW Government's [Identifying AI](/policies/nsw-identifying-ai-guidance) guidance now directs them there. The [page](https://www.digital.nsw.gov.au/policy/artificial-intelligence/nsw-artificial-intelligence-assessment-framework/identifying-ai) states that "From 30 September 2026, the AIAF Platform is the authoritative channel for AI use case registration and assessment across NSW Government."

## What it says

The guidance is for public servants involved in the lifecycle of digital solutions. It is "not an exhaustive source for determining whether AI is present or whether an AIAF assessment is required" ([Identifying AI](https://www.digital.nsw.gov.au/policy/artificial-intelligence/nsw-artificial-intelligence-assessment-framework/identifying-ai)).

It offers a set of contrasts between AI and conventional software, and a questionnaire. A system is unlikely to use AI if the answer is yes to all eight "non-AI" questions, and likely to use AI if the answer is yes to any of seven "AI" questions.

| If the solution | The agency |
| --- | --- |
| uses or may use AI | registers the use case in the AIAF Platform, and "The registration process will determine whether an AIAF assessment is required." |
| does not use AI | documents the decision under its governance and records-management requirements, and reviews it if the solution's features or use change |

An agency without platform access because its SectorLink onboarding is incomplete can request an offline assessment from NSW GovAI. The [AIAF page](https://www.digital.nsw.gov.au/policy/artificial-intelligence/ai-governance-assurance-and-frameworks/nsw-ai-assessment-framework) states that this pathway "is not a general alternative to the platform and cannot be used by preference".

## What changed

The guidance dates from December 2024 ([Identifying AI](/policies/nsw-identifying-ai-guidance)). Its current text reflects the platform requirement that applies from 30 September 2026 ([AIAF page](https://www.digital.nsw.gov.au/policy/artificial-intelligence/ai-governance-assurance-and-frameworks/nsw-ai-assessment-framework)). The page now carries no date. The register keeps December 2024, at month precision, from the filename of the original download, which the page no longer links.

## Who it affects

NSW Government agencies and the public servants who buy, build and manage their digital solutions. The [NSW AI Operational Policy](/policies/nsw-ai-operational-policy) requires agencies to "Ensure that all AI use cases are registered in the AI Assessment Framework Platform" and to apply the [AI Assessment Framework](/policies/nsw-artificial-intelligence-assessment-framework) "where the registration process determines an assessment is required". The [policy page](https://www.digital.nsw.gov.au/policy/artificial-intelligence/ai-strategy-and-operational-policy/nsw-ai-operational-policy) refers readers to Circular DCS-2026-02 for the entities it covers. High- and critical-risk use cases go to the AI Secretariat for review by the NSW AI Review Committee ([AIAF page](https://www.digital.nsw.gov.au/policy/artificial-intelligence/ai-governance-assurance-and-frameworks/nsw-ai-assessment-framework)).

## Analysis

In Policai's analysis, the agency no longer decides alone whether an assessment is needed: it registers the use case, and the registration process decides. Every use case that may involve AI therefore passes through one government-wide channel. On the guidance's own test, many everyday products with built-in transcription, summarisation or chat features could need registering.

Two official pages describe the transition differently. The [AIAF page](https://www.digital.nsw.gov.au/policy/artificial-intelligence/ai-governance-assurance-and-frameworks/nsw-ai-assessment-framework) says agencies "must use the AIAF Platform for all new AI use case registrations and assessments" from 30 September 2026. The [Operational Policy page](https://www.digital.nsw.gov.au/policy/artificial-intelligence/ai-strategy-and-operational-policy/nsw-ai-operational-policy) says agencies "must continue to apply the existing AI Assessment Framework Excel-based assessment process to all AI use cases until the platform is fully adopted". Neither says how existing Excel assessments move to the platform. Agencies may want to confirm with NSW GovAI.
