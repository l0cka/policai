The [Online Safety Amendment (Digital Duty of Care) Bill 2026](/policies/federal-online-safety-amendment-digital-duty-of-care-bill-2026) finally passed both Houses on 25 November 2026 ([APH bill page](https://www.aph.gov.au/Parliamentary_Business/Bills_Legislation/Bills_Search_Results/Result?bId=r7553)). The Senate agreed to the third reading that day with 42 government and 10 Australian Greens amendments, and the House of Representatives agreed to the Senate amendments the same day ([APH bill page](https://www.aph.gov.au/Parliamentary_Business/Bills_Legislation/Bills_Search_Results/Result?bId=r7553)). The bill page records no Royal Assent yet ([APH bill page](https://www.aph.gov.au/Parliamentary_Business/Bills_Legislation/Bills_Search_Results/Result?bId=r7553)). The Minister for Communications, Anika Wells, said the duty "will come into effect in 12 months" ([media release, 25 November 2026](https://minister.infrastructure.gov.au/wells/media-release/australias-digital-duty-care-becomes-law)).

## What it says

The government amendments were made "to address feedback raised during the Senate Environment and Communications Legislation Committee's inquiry", and they include a private cause of action, privacy protections for age assurance and the extension of infringement notices to the duty ([supplementary explanatory memorandum, sheet AO108](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/ems/r7553_ems_523cbc6b-3131-420b-a8fa-8dcf21e1a400/upload_pdf/Supplementary%20EM_JC019474.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf)). The amendments made by the Senate include ([schedule of amendments](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/sched/r7553_sched_d553a5b5-e374-4b45-abb5-fa1b25ed0eec/upload_pdf/Online%20Safety%20Amendment%20%28Digital%20Duty%20of%20Care%29%20Bill%202026.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf)):

- **A private cause of action.** New section 28D gives an individual a cause of action against a relevant duty holder if they suffer loss or damage "as a result of the relevant duty holder failing to comply with their digital duty of care", and a relevant duty holder includes "a service that generates material using artificial intelligence and that uses an internet carriage service in the course of doing so" ([schedule, item 44](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/sched/r7553_sched_d553a5b5-e374-4b45-abb5-fa1b25ed0eec/upload_pdf/Online%20Safety%20Amendment%20%28Digital%20Duty%20of%20Care%29%20Bill%202026.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf)).
- **Remedies, costs and time limits.** A court may award damages, an injunction or an order to apologise, and damages may include damages for emotional distress and, "in exceptional circumstances", exemplary or punitive damages ([schedule, item 44, section 28F](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/sched/r7553_sched_d553a5b5-e374-4b45-abb5-fa1b25ed0eec/upload_pdf/Online%20Safety%20Amendment%20%28Digital%20Duty%20of%20Care%29%20Bill%202026.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf)). A successful individual's costs must be paid by the duty holders they succeed against, and an individual may be ordered to pay costs only in limited cases such as vexatious proceedings ([schedule, item 44, section 28G](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/sched/r7553_sched_d553a5b5-e374-4b45-abb5-fa1b25ed0eec/upload_pdf/Online%20Safety%20Amendment%20%28Digital%20Duty%20of%20Care%29%20Bill%202026.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf)). Proceedings must start before the later of 21 years after the individual's birth and 6 years after the loss or damage ([schedule, item 44, section 28E](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/sched/r7553_sched_d553a5b5-e374-4b45-abb5-fa1b25ed0eec/upload_pdf/Online%20Safety%20Amendment%20%28Digital%20Duty%20of%20Care%29%20Bill%202026.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf)).
- **Higher penalties.** Greens amendments set the civil penalty for a body corporate at the greater of 300,000 penalty units and 5% of global turnover, including for failing to conduct, make available or retain risk assessments ([schedule, items 29 and 34](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/sched/r7553_sched_d553a5b5-e374-4b45-abb5-fa1b25ed0eec/upload_pdf/Online%20Safety%20Amendment%20%28Digital%20Duty%20of%20Care%29%20Bill%202026.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf)). The Minister puts the maximum at "5% of the offending company's global turnover, or $109.2 million – whichever is greater" ([media release](https://minister.infrastructure.gov.au/wells/media-release/australias-digital-duty-care-becomes-law)).
- **Addictive design.** A Greens amendment adds "any design features of the service that result, or could result, in addictive behaviour" to the matters in section 27 ([schedule, item 30](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/sched/r7553_sched_d553a5b5-e374-4b45-abb5-fa1b25ed0eec/upload_pdf/Online%20Safety%20Amendment%20%28Digital%20Duty%20of%20Care%29%20Bill%202026.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf)).
- **An online safety ombudsperson.** New section 27GA lets the legislative rules provide for an ombudsperson to receive and investigate complaints about social media services, relevant electronic services, designated internet services and services that generate material using AI ([schedule, item 42](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/sched/r7553_sched_d553a5b5-e374-4b45-abb5-fa1b25ed0eec/upload_pdf/Online%20Safety%20Amendment%20%28Digital%20Duty%20of%20Care%29%20Bill%202026.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf)).
- **Fake nude apps.** The eSafety Commissioner may give a hosting service provider a removal notice, and may request or require an internet service provider to block access, for an app or website designed for the predominant purpose of generating fake nude material or predominantly used to generate it ([schedule, items 2 to 7](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/sched/r7553_sched_d553a5b5-e374-4b45-abb5-fa1b25ed0eec/upload_pdf/Online%20Safety%20Amendment%20%28Digital%20Duty%20of%20Care%29%20Bill%202026.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf)).

## Who it affects

The Minister says online services "such as games, apps, social media platforms and AI chatbots" must protect Australians from seriously harmful material and conduct ([media release](https://minister.infrastructure.gov.au/wells/media-release/australias-digital-duty-care-becomes-law)). The bill places the duty on online services, including services that generate material using AI ([record](/policies/federal-online-safety-amendment-digital-duty-of-care-bill-2026)). The private cause of action is narrower than the duty: it runs only against the four kinds of relevant duty holder listed in section 28D(3), one of which is AI generation services ([schedule, item 44](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/sched/r7553_sched_d553a5b5-e374-4b45-abb5-fa1b25ed0eec/upload_pdf/Online%20Safety%20Amendment%20%28Digital%20Duty%20of%20Care%29%20Bill%202026.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf)).

Providers of [registered eSafety codes for generative AI services and AI companion chatbots](/policies/esafety-genai-industry-codes) remain under those codes for now, because the bill would repeal them 12 months after Royal Assent ([record](/policies/esafety-genai-industry-codes)).

## Analysis

In Policai's analysis, the Senate settled the question the committee left open. The committee recommended a civil right of action without saying whether AI services were in scope, and the passed amendment names them alongside social media, messaging and other internet services ([schedule, item 44](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/sched/r7553_sched_d553a5b5-e374-4b45-abb5-fa1b25ed0eec/upload_pdf/Online%20Safety%20Amendment%20%28Digital%20Duty%20of%20Care%29%20Bill%202026.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf)). That makes AI generation services one of the few categories a person can sue under the duty directly, not only through eSafety enforcement.

In Policai's analysis, the costs rule and the long limitation period for children matter as much as the cause of action itself. A claimant who wins recovers costs and rarely bears the provider's costs, and a child's claim can be brought until they turn 21 ([schedule, item 44, sections 28E and 28G](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/sched/r7553_sched_d553a5b5-e374-4b45-abb5-fa1b25ed0eec/upload_pdf/Online%20Safety%20Amendment%20%28Digital%20Duty%20of%20Care%29%20Bill%202026.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf)). For AI companion and chatbot providers, the duty will be tested in court by individuals as well as by the regulator.

In Policai's analysis, two parts of the AI picture are still to come. The ombudsperson depends on legislative rules that have not been made ([schedule, item 42](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/sched/r7553_sched_d553a5b5-e374-4b45-abb5-fa1b25ed0eec/upload_pdf/Online%20Safety%20Amendment%20%28Digital%20Duty%20of%20Care%29%20Bill%202026.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf)), and the duty starts 12 months out, on the Minister's account ([media release](https://minister.infrastructure.gov.au/wells/media-release/australias-digital-duty-care-becomes-law)). The register record will move from proposed once the bill receives Royal Assent.
