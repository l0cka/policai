The Australian Federal Police (AFP) has published its [Artificial Intelligence Strategy 2026-28](/policies/afp-ai-strategy-2026-28), with the text on a [publication page](https://www.afp.gov.au/news-centre/publications/artificial-intelligence-strategy-2026-28) and in a [PDF](https://www.afp.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-09/Artificial-Intelligence-Strategy.pdf). The register records publication on 2 September 2026 ([record](/policies/afp-ai-strategy-2026-28)). In the Commissioner's foreword, Commissioner Krissy Barrett APM says the strategy commits the AFP to a human-led, responsible and transparent approach to AI, that "AI will support, not replace, the judgement of AFP members", and that "Accountability for decisions always rests with the AFP" ([publication page](https://www.afp.gov.au/news-centre/publications/artificial-intelligence-strategy-2026-28)).

## What it says

The vision is to establish the AFP as a collaborative leader that brings law enforcement and the broader AI community together in the applied, responsible use of AI ([publication page](https://www.afp.gov.au/news-centre/publications/artificial-intelligence-strategy-2026-28)).

The strategy sets five strategic goals: enhance productivity; strengthen data-driven decision-making; safeguard member wellbeing; empower creativity and workforce engagement; and counter the criminal threat from AI, including deepfakes, synthetic media and disinformation ([publication page](https://www.afp.gov.au/news-centre/publications/artificial-intelligence-strategy-2026-28)). Productivity examples include transcription, translation, summarisation, video analytics and entity recognition ([publication page](https://www.afp.gov.au/news-centre/publications/artificial-intelligence-strategy-2026-28)).

Four adoption pillars frame the work: People (AI knowledge, culture and leadership; human-led decision-making; safeguarding member wellbeing), Process (responsible AI governance and policies; operational alignment), Technology (investment in technology; end-to-end data management) and Partnerships (partnership and collaboration; public trust and transparency) ([publication page](https://www.afp.gov.au/news-centre/publications/artificial-intelligence-strategy-2026-28)).

The AFP says it has fully adopted the guiding principles of the Australia New Zealand Policing Advisory Agency (ANZPAA) Responsible and Ethical Artificial Intelligence Framework. It lists those principles as explainability, accountability, human oversight, fairness, transparency, skills and knowledge, proportionality and justifiability, reliability, and privacy and security ([publication page](https://www.afp.gov.au/news-centre/publications/artificial-intelligence-strategy-2026-28)). It names [Australia's AI Ethics Principles](/policies/australias-ai-ethics-framework), the [National framework for the assurance of artificial intelligence in government](/policies/national-framework-assurance-ai-government), the [Policy for the Responsible Use of AI in Government](/policies/policy-responsible-use-ai-government-v2) and the [Voluntary AI Safety Standard](/policies/voluntary-ai-safety-standard) among others informing its approach ([publication page](https://www.afp.gov.au/news-centre/publications/artificial-intelligence-strategy-2026-28)).

The risks it names are AI system failure; overuse, inappropriate or reckless use; legal, ethical and privacy risk; reputational and public trust risk; and bias and hallucination ([publication page](https://www.afp.gov.au/news-centre/publications/artificial-intelligence-strategy-2026-28)). On measuring success, it says the AFP will establish mechanisms to measure progress and evaluate impact across all strategic pillars; the register records that the strategy gives no specific measures ([publication page](https://www.afp.gov.au/news-centre/publications/artificial-intelligence-strategy-2026-28); [record](/policies/afp-ai-strategy-2026-28)).

## Who it affects

The strategy is the AFP's plan for its own adoption of AI. The Commissioner's foreword addresses every member of the AFP and the AFP's partners ([publication page](https://www.afp.gov.au/news-centre/publications/artificial-intelligence-strategy-2026-28)). The partnerships pillar names state and territory policing, Commonwealth law enforcement agencies, national security agencies, academia and industry ([publication page](https://www.afp.gov.au/news-centre/publications/artificial-intelligence-strategy-2026-28)). The AFP says differences may exist in the use and assurance of AI across Australian state and territory policing jurisdictions, and that those variations will be respected ([publication page](https://www.afp.gov.au/news-centre/publications/artificial-intelligence-strategy-2026-28)).

## Analysis

In Policai's analysis, the strategy is an agency adoption plan for the AFP, not a rule that binds the public or industry. Barrett's foreword places accountability with the AFP and keeps human judgement central to AI-assisted work ([publication page](https://www.afp.gov.au/news-centre/publications/artificial-intelligence-strategy-2026-28)).

In Policai's analysis, the named Commonwealth instruments are presented as sources that inform the AFP's approach; the strategy does not say that following it meets those instruments ([publication page](https://www.afp.gov.au/news-centre/publications/artificial-intelligence-strategy-2026-28)). The register records the [Voluntary AI Safety Standard](/policies/voluntary-ai-safety-standard) as superseded.

In Policai's analysis, measurement is the open question: the strategy says the AFP will establish mechanisms to measure progress and evaluate impact across all strategic pillars and does not publish specific measures ([publication page](https://www.afp.gov.au/news-centre/publications/artificial-intelligence-strategy-2026-28); [record](/policies/afp-ai-strategy-2026-28)).
