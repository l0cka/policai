On 30 April 2026 the Australian Prudential Regulation Authority (APRA) wrote to all APRA-regulated entities calling for "a step-change in how banks, insurers and superannuation trustees manage AI-related risks" ([APRA letter](https://www.apra.gov.au/apra-letter-to-industry-on-artificial-intelligence-ai)). The letter reports what APRA found in a targeted engagement with selected large banks, insurers and superannuation trustees in late 2025, and sets out what it expects ([record](/policies/apra-ai-industry-letter-2026)).

## What it says

APRA found that "assurance practices are not keeping pace with the scale, speed and complexity of AI", and that many boards "are still developing the technical literacy required to provide effective challenge" ([APRA letter](https://www.apra.gov.au/apra-letter-to-industry-on-artificial-intelligence-ai)). It expects boards, at a minimum, to understand AI well enough to set strategic direction and provide "effective challenge and oversight", and to oversee an AI strategy consistent with the entity's risk appetite, with monitoring, reporting and defined triggers for action.

An attachment for accountable executives sets minimum expectations in four areas ([APRA letter](https://www.apra.gov.au/apra-letter-to-industry-on-artificial-intelligence-ai)):

- **Information security:** controls over agentic and autonomous workflows, timely patching, security testing of AI-generated code, and credible fallback processes where AI supports critical operations.
- **Governance:** an inventory of AI tools and use cases, ownership across the AI lifecycle, and "human involvement for high-risk decisions and accountability".
- **Suppliers:** a map of the AI supply chain, including fourth-party dependencies, and active management of concentration risk, including exit arrangements for critical AI providers.
- **Assurance:** continuous monitoring proportionate to the use case, and second-line risk and internal audit functions able to assess AI systems independently.

The letter says APRA's prudential framework is "technology and vendor agnostic". Where entities fail to manage AI risks adequately, APRA "will take stronger supervisory action and, where appropriate, pursue enforcement" ([APRA letter](https://www.apra.gov.au/apra-letter-to-industry-on-artificial-intelligence-ai)). It also says APRA is engaging the sector on cyber threats from "high capability AI frontier models such as Anthropic Mythos".

In August 2026 APRA and the Australian Securities and Investments Commission (ASIC) published a joint paper ([record](/policies/asic-apra-resilience-at-frontier-ai-speed-2026)) on nine industry roundtables held in June and July 2026, attended by more than 380 entities ([p 1](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/imunrchk/asic-and-apra-resilience-at-frontier-ai-speed-roundatable.pdf)). The regulators expect entities "to take action now" and to show that their decisions, escalation, recovery, assurance and governance "can operate at the speed required by emerging frontier AI threats" ([p 3](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/imunrchk/asic-and-apra-resilience-at-frontier-ai-speed-roundatable.pdf)).

## Who it affects

The letter is addressed to all APRA-regulated entities. APRA says the lessons from the larger entities "will assist other entities who may be earlier in their AI adoption journey" ([APRA letter](https://www.apra.gov.au/apra-letter-to-industry-on-artificial-intelligence-ai)). The roundtables also included market intermediaries, payment providers, mortgage brokers, financial advisers, credit providers and regulated entities' material service providers ([APRA–ASIC paper, p 1](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/imunrchk/asic-and-apra-resilience-at-frontier-ai-speed-roundatable.pdf)).

## Analysis

In Policai's analysis, the letter creates no new prudential standard. It states how APRA reads its existing framework for AI, and APRA will supervise against that reading. The expectations most likely to need new work are tested exit arrangements for critical AI providers and continuous monitoring of models, because APRA reports that few of the large entities it examined had them.

APRA says it is finalising its forward plan for supervising AI risk and will consider whether further policy action is needed ([APRA letter](https://www.apra.gov.au/apra-letter-to-industry-on-artificial-intelligence-ai)). Until that plan is public, the reference points are the letter and the standards the joint paper lists, CPS 230 Operational Risk Management and CPS 234 Information Security ([p 4](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/imunrchk/asic-and-apra-resilience-at-frontier-ai-speed-roundatable.pdf)).
