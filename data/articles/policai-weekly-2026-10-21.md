The Online Safety Amendment (Digital Duty of Care) Bill 2026 was introduced in the House of Representatives on 15 October 2026 and referred the same day to the Senate Environment and Communications Legislation Committee, which is due to report on 20 November ([Parliament of Australia](https://www.aph.gov.au/Parliamentary_Business/Bills_Legislation/Bills_Search_Results/Result?bId=r7553)). This briefing covers the seven days to 21 October 2026. Collection has not completed a healthy run since 18 October, so [This week](/this-week) does not cover the whole window, and each item was checked at its official source.

## What changed

- **Digital duty of care.** The bill's Schedule 3 would repeal Division 7 of Part 9 of the Online Safety Act 2021, which supports the industry codes and standards, after a 12-month transition ([bill as introduced](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/bills/r7553_first-reps/toc_word/26131b01.docx)). Those include the [codes covering generative AI and AI companions](/policies/esafety-genai-industry-codes). Policai's [instrument note](/analysis/digital-duty-of-care-bill-2026) sets out the detail.
- **ASD Essentials.** On 15 October the Australian Signals Directorate (ASD) launched the ASD Essentials, "an evolution of Australia's widely adopted Essential Eight framework", with chapters for enterprise IT, operational technology and cloud ([news item](https://www.cyber.gov.au/news/asd-essentials-evolving-our-approach-to-cyber-security-resilience)). Its [AI guidance](https://www.cyber.gov.au/publication/ai-considerations-within-the-essentials), published the same day, says the framework "applies regardless of whether an organisation consumes, develops or deploys artificial intelligence (AI)".
- **Fair Work Commission.** The [guidance note on generative AI](/policies/fwc-genai-guidance-note) commenced on 20 October ([guidance note, para 5](https://www.fwc.gov.au/documents/resources/guidance-note-use-of-generative-ai-in-commission-cases.pdf)).
- **Senate data centres inquiry.** On 20 October the committee tabled a progress report that updates its reporting date to 23 March 2027 ([inquiry page](https://www.aph.gov.au/Parliamentary_Business/Committees/Senate/Environment_and_Communications/AIdatacentres48P)).
- **Joint Select Committee on AI.** The committee held public hearings in Canberra on 16 October and in Sydney on 20 and 21 October ([hearings page](https://www.aph.gov.au/Parliamentary_Business/Committees/Joint/Artificial_Intelligence/ArtificialIntelligence/Public_Hearings)).
- **APS bargaining.** Verified in this window, but not new. The Australian Public Service Commission (APSC) page, last updated on 9 October, says the Chief Negotiator "is not proposing a standalone common condition on AI", and would instead include AI in the Learning and Development and Consultative Committee common conditions ([APSC](https://www.apsc.gov.au/apsbargaining/proposed-commonwealth-positions/further-key-employment-conditions)).

## Why it matters

In Policai's analysis, the bill and ASD's framework both treat AI as part of wider systems rather than a separate category. The bill would replace AI-specific online-safety codes with a general duty, and ASD folds AI into its general cyber controls. ASD says: "Until associated regulatory changes are communicated by regulators, organisations should continue to meet their current regulatory obligations" ([news item](https://www.cyber.gov.au/news/asd-essentials-evolving-our-approach-to-cyber-security-resilience)). Organisations that report Essential Eight maturity to a regulator may want to check which framework that regulator expects.

The Joint Select Committee's report is now due almost four months before the Senate data centres report. Its early advice recommended nationally consistent standards for data centres, implemented through state and territory planning laws ([committee letter](https://static.aph.gov.au/-/media/Committees/ai_ctte/JSC_AI_letter_20261012_Final.pdf)).
