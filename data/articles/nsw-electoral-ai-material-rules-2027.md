New South Wales has added two offences for artificial intelligence (AI) generated electoral material to the Electoral Act 2017. The Electoral Legislation Amendment (Elections) Act 2026 (Act No 6 of 2026) inserted sections 189A and 189B, which commenced on assent on 7 April 2026 ([NSW Parliament](https://www.parliament.nsw.gov.au/parliamentary-business/bills/bill-details?billId=18875); [record](/policies/nsw-electoral-legislation-amendment-elections-act-2026); [s 2(d)](https://files.parliament.nsw.gov.au/fileapi/ParlFiles/GetArtifact?serverRelativeUrl=%2Fbill%2Ffiles%2F18875%2FPassed%20by%20both%20Houses.pdf)). The 13 March 2027 State election will be the first held under them ([NSW Electoral Commission](https://elections.nsw.gov.au/elections/state-elections/2027-nsw-state-election)).

## What it says

Both offences turn on "digitally generated electoral material": electoral material with audio visual, visual or audio content "generated substantially, or modified or altered significantly, by artificial intelligence". Cartoons, animated drawings and material the regulations prescribe are excluded ([s 189A(4)](https://files.parliament.nsw.gov.au/fileapi/ParlFiles/GetArtifact?serverRelativeUrl=%2Fbill%2Ffiles%2F18875%2FPassed%20by%20both%20Houses.pdf)).

- **Depicting real people (s 189A):** during the regulated period, a person must not print, publish, distribute or publicly display such material depicting a simulated person "performing an act that the real person depicted in the depiction did not perform". A simulated person purports to be a particular real person, or resembles them so closely that a reasonable person would think it likely to be them. Maximum penalty for an individual: 60 penalty units, 6 months' imprisonment, or both (300 penalty units otherwise) ([s 189A(1), (4)](https://files.parliament.nsw.gov.au/fileapi/ParlFiles/GetArtifact?serverRelativeUrl=%2Fbill%2Ffiles%2F18875%2FPassed%20by%20both%20Houses.pdf)).
- **Labelling (s 189B):** material depicting a digitally created person, event or place that a reasonable person would think real must state that it is digitally generated electoral material, in the same form as the content (written, spoken, or both for audio visual). Maximum penalty for an individual: 20 penalty units, 6 months' imprisonment, or both (100 penalty units otherwise) ([s 189B(1)](https://files.parliament.nsw.gov.au/fileapi/ParlFiles/GetArtifact?serverRelativeUrl=%2Fbill%2Ffiles%2F18875%2FPassed%20by%20both%20Houses.pdf)).
- **Defences:** for s 189A, the real person gave written consent. For both sections, the defendant took no part in determining the content and could not reasonably have known it contravened the section. Consent given by a person under 17 or with a cognitive impairment, or obtained by duress or deception, is not effective ([ss 189A(2)–(3), 189B(3)](https://files.parliament.nsw.gov.au/fileapi/ParlFiles/GetArtifact?serverRelativeUrl=%2Fbill%2Ffiles%2F18875%2FPassed%20by%20both%20Houses.pdf)).

The NSW Electoral Commission expects the regulated period to run from the issue of writs on 22 February 2027 to election day ([NSW Electoral Commission](https://elections.nsw.gov.au/elections/state-elections/2027-nsw-state-election)).

## Who it affects

Both offences apply to "a person", not only candidates or parties ([ss 189A(1), 189B(1)](https://files.parliament.nsw.gov.au/fileapi/ParlFiles/GetArtifact?serverRelativeUrl=%2Fbill%2Ffiles%2F18875%2FPassed%20by%20both%20Houses.pdf)).

The Commission is building a compliance framework, with guidance due before the election. It will seek take-down or reporting arrangements with social media platforms where possible, and use a 2024 referral protocol with the eSafety Commissioner if a platform ignores a take-down request ([change summary, p 14](https://elections.nsw.gov.au/getmedia/63322be6-be72-46e6-8783-1927930608fb/2027-nsw-state-election-change-summary.pdf)).

South Australia has regulated AI-generated electoral advertising since 10 October 2025, including simulated depictions and disclosure statements, with withdrawal or correction powers ([record](/policies/sa-electoral-ai-generated-advertisements)).

## Analysis

In Policai's analysis, this is binding AI-specific law, still rare in Australia. It regulates the output, not the tool: what the material shows and when it appears.

In Policai's analysis, the offences are narrower than a general deepfake ban. They reach only depictions of real people doing things they did not do, or material that would pass as real, and only in a regulated period of about three weeks.

In Policai's analysis, unlike the South Australian scheme, neither section gives the Electoral Commissioner a power to order withdrawal or correction. Negotiated take-down with platforms is likely to carry much of the practical weight. The main open question for the Commission's guidance is lightly edited footage, where "altered significantly" may be tested.
