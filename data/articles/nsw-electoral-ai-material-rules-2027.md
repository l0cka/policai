New South Wales has added two offences for artificial intelligence (AI) generated electoral material to the Electoral Act 2017. The Electoral Legislation Amendment (Elections) Act 2026 (Act No 6 of 2026) was assented to on 7 April 2026 ([NSW Parliament](https://www.parliament.nsw.gov.au/parliamentary-business/bills/bill-details?billId=18875)), and the new sections 189A and 189B commenced that day ([record](/policies/nsw-electoral-legislation-amendment-elections-act-2026); [s 2(d)](https://files.parliament.nsw.gov.au/fileapi/ParlFiles/GetArtifact?serverRelativeUrl=%2Fbill%2Ffiles%2F18875%2FPassed%20by%20both%20Houses.pdf)). The 2027 NSW State election, on Saturday 13 March 2027, will be the first general election held under them ([NSW Electoral Commission](https://elections.nsw.gov.au/elections/state-elections/2027-nsw-state-election)).

## What it says

Both offences turn on "digitally generated electoral material": electoral material with audio visual, visual or audio content that is "generated substantially, or modified or altered significantly, by artificial intelligence". Cartoons and animated drawings are excluded, as is material of a kind the regulations prescribe ([s 189A(4)](https://files.parliament.nsw.gov.au/fileapi/ParlFiles/GetArtifact?serverRelativeUrl=%2Fbill%2Ffiles%2F18875%2FPassed%20by%20both%20Houses.pdf)).

- **Depicting real people (s 189A):** a person must not, during the regulated period, print, publish, distribute or publicly display such material if it depicts a simulated person "performing an act that the real person depicted in the depiction did not perform". A simulated person is one who purports to be, or so closely resembles, a particular real person that a reasonable person would consider it likely to be them. The maximum penalty is 60 penalty units or 6 months' imprisonment, or both, for an individual, and 300 penalty units otherwise ([s 189A(1), (4)](https://files.parliament.nsw.gov.au/fileapi/ParlFiles/GetArtifact?serverRelativeUrl=%2Fbill%2Ffiles%2F18875%2FPassed%20by%20both%20Houses.pdf)).
- **Consent and other defences:** it is a defence that the real person gave written consent, or that the defendant took no part in determining the content and could not reasonably be expected to have known it contravened the section. Consent is not effective if given by a person under 17 or with a cognitive impairment, or obtained by duress or deception ([s 189A(2)–(3)](https://files.parliament.nsw.gov.au/fileapi/ParlFiles/GetArtifact?serverRelativeUrl=%2Fbill%2Ffiles%2F18875%2FPassed%20by%20both%20Houses.pdf)).
- **Labelling (s 189B):** material that depicts a digitally created person, event or place that a reasonable person would think is real must carry a statement that it is digitally generated electoral material. The statement must be written and spoken for audio visual content, written for visual content and spoken for audio content. The maximum penalty is 20 penalty units or 6 months' imprisonment, or both, for an individual, and 100 penalty units otherwise ([s 189B(1)](https://files.parliament.nsw.gov.au/fileapi/ParlFiles/GetArtifact?serverRelativeUrl=%2Fbill%2Ffiles%2F18875%2FPassed%20by%20both%20Houses.pdf)). The same no-part-in-the-content defence applies ([s 189B(3)](https://files.parliament.nsw.gov.au/fileapi/ParlFiles/GetArtifact?serverRelativeUrl=%2Fbill%2Ffiles%2F18875%2FPassed%20by%20both%20Houses.pdf)).
- **No late carve-outs:** the Minister must not recommend a regulation excluding a kind of material from the definition between 1 October in the year before a general election and election day, or during a by-election writ period ([s 189A(5)](https://files.parliament.nsw.gov.au/fileapi/ParlFiles/GetArtifact?serverRelativeUrl=%2Fbill%2Ffiles%2F18875%2FPassed%20by%20both%20Houses.pdf)).

For the 2027 election, the NSW Electoral Commission expects writs to issue on Monday 22 February 2027, when the regulated period for electoral material begins, and the period to end on election day ([NSW Electoral Commission](https://elections.nsw.gov.au/elections/state-elections/2027-nsw-state-election)).

## Who it affects

Both offences apply to "a person", not only to candidates or parties ([ss 189A(1), 189B(1)](https://files.parliament.nsw.gov.au/fileapi/ParlFiles/GetArtifact?serverRelativeUrl=%2Fbill%2Ffiles%2F18875%2FPassed%20by%20both%20Houses.pdf)). The NSW Electoral Commission lists them among the new offences for the 2027 election ([Bulletin No 2](https://elections.nsw.gov.au/bulletins/2027-nsw-state-election-bulletin-no-2)).

The Commission says it is building a compliance and enforcement framework for the provisions, with more guidance to be published before the election ([change summary, p 14](https://elections.nsw.gov.au/getmedia/63322be6-be72-46e6-8783-1927930608fb/2027-nsw-state-election-change-summary.pdf)). It also says it will engage social media platforms to agree, where possible, on take-down or reporting mechanisms, and work with the eSafety Commissioner on a 2024 referral protocol if a platform does not comply with a take-down request ([change summary, p 14](https://elections.nsw.gov.au/getmedia/63322be6-be72-46e6-8783-1927930608fb/2027-nsw-state-election-change-summary.pdf)).

South Australia has regulated AI-generated electoral advertising since 10 October 2025, covering simulated depictions of real people and disclosure statements, with withdrawal or correction powers ([record](/policies/sa-electoral-ai-generated-advertisements)).

## Analysis

In Policai's analysis, this is binding AI-specific law, which remains rare in Australia. It regulates the output, not the tool: liability depends on what the material shows and when it is published, not on which system made it.

In Policai's analysis, the two offences are narrower than a general deepfake ban. Section 189A reaches only depictions of a real person doing something they did not do, and section 189B only material a reasonable person would think shows something real. Both apply only within the regulated period, which for the 2027 election is expected to run for about three weeks. AI material published before the writs issue is outside both offences.

In Policai's analysis, sections 189A and 189B do not themselves give the Electoral Commissioner a power to order withdrawal or correction, unlike the South Australian scheme. The Commission's stated approach of negotiated take-down with platforms is likely to carry much of the practical weight. How it treats lightly edited footage, where "altered significantly" may be tested, is the main open question for its forthcoming guidance.
