This briefing covers developments Policai detected and verified from
30 September to 7 October 2026. Policai's collection has not completed a
fully healthy run since 7 October, so this is the latest complete window on
[This week](/this-week), not the past seven days. On 30 September the Office
of the Australian Information Commissioner (OAIC) published
[guidance on the APP 1.7–1.9 transparency obligation](/policies/oaic-app-1-7-adm-transparency-guidance)
for decisions made or informed by computer programs.

## What changed

**OAIC guidance on automated decision-making transparency.** From
10 December 2026, an entity covered by the Australian Privacy Principles must
set out in its privacy policy the kinds of personal information and the kinds
of decisions involved when it uses personal information in a computer program
to make, or do something substantially and directly related to making, a
decision that could reasonably be expected to significantly affect an
individual's rights or interests. The
[OAIC fact sheet](https://www.oaic.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0021/269013/APP-1.7-1.9-Transparency-Obligation-Fact-Sheet.PDF)
says a computer program includes "pre-programmed rule-based processes",
machine learning, ordinary software and generative AI. It also says a decision
may be in scope "even where a computer program output does not replace the
entire decision-making process or is subject to human review".

**Senate inquiry into artificial intelligence and data centres.** Policai
verified this inquiry in the window, but it is not new. The Senate referred it
to the Environment and Communications References Committee on 13 May 2026.
The [inquiry page](https://www.aph.gov.au/Parliamentary_Business/Committees/Senate/Environment_and_Communications/AIdatacentres48P)
lists past public hearings at Lane Cove on 22 September and Yarraville on
23 September 2026, a hearing in Darwin on 3 November 2026, and a reporting
date of 16 November 2026. Submissions have closed.

## Coming up

- **20 October 2026:** the Fair Work Commission's
  [guidance note on generative AI](/policies/fwc-genai-guidance-note)
  commences.
- **10 December 2026:** the
  [APP 1.7–1.9 transparency obligation](/policies/oaic-app-1-7-adm-transparency-guidance)
  applies.

From 20 October, a person who uses generative AI to prepare a document for a
Commission case must say so and say how it was used. They must check the
document and state that they have checked it. Under the
[guidance note](https://www.fwc.gov.au/documents/resources/guidance-note-use-of-generative-ai-in-commission-cases.pdf),
that check "must be done by a person" and cannot be delegated to a generative
AI tool. Using generative AI only to check spelling or grammar, or to format a
document, is excluded.

## Why it matters

In Policai's analysis, the OAIC's reading of "computer program" is the point
most likely to catch organisations out. The fact sheet's first worked example
is a spreadsheet formula that ranks clients for health and aged care
services, and the OAIC treats that as in scope. An organisation that reviews
only its "AI" systems before 10 December may miss rule-based tools that do the
same work. The OAIC advises entities in doubt to "take a cautious approach"
and include the information in their privacy policy.

The Fair Work Commission's rules apply to everyone who lodges documents, not
only lawyers. Self-represented parties who use a chatbot to draft an
application will need to add the disclosure and the checking statement. The
exclusion for spelling, grammar and formatting is narrow. A tool that rewrites
or summarises text is outside it.

On data centres, two federal processes now run side by side. The
[PM&C consultation](/policies/pmc-ai-infrastructure-consultation-2026) on AI
infrastructure closed on 9 October 2026. The
[consultation page](https://www.pmc.gov.au/domestic-policy/office-ai/getting-it-right-building-ai-infrastructure-works-australia)
states that the Government will work with the states and territories to
create mandatory standards for how data centres use energy, water and land,
and then plans to legislate them. The Senate committee is due to report on
16 November. Readers following data centre regulation should watch for both.
