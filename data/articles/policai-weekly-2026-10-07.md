From 10 December 2026, an entity covered by the Australian Privacy Principles must describe in its privacy policy the kinds of personal information and the kinds of decisions involved when a computer program makes, or does something substantially and directly related to making, a decision that could reasonably be expected to significantly affect an individual's rights or interests. The OAIC published [guidance on the new duty](/policies/oaic-app-1-7-adm-transparency-guidance) on 30 September 2026. This is the latest complete window on [This week](/this-week), 30 September to 7 October: collection has not had a fully healthy run since 7 October.

## What changed

- **OAIC guidance on automated decision-making.** The [fact sheet](https://www.oaic.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0021/269013/APP-1.7-1.9-Transparency-Obligation-Fact-Sheet.PDF) says a computer program includes "pre-programmed rule-based processes", machine learning, ordinary software and generative AI. A decision may be in scope "even where a computer program output does not replace the entire decision-making process or is subject to human review".
- **Senate inquiry into AI and data centres.** Verified in this window, but not new. The Senate referred it on 13 May 2026. Submissions closed on 1 September 2026, and the committee is due to report on 16 November 2026 ([inquiry page](https://www.aph.gov.au/Parliamentary_Business/Committees/Senate/Environment_and_Communications/AIdatacentres48P)).

## Coming up

| Date | What |
| --- | --- |
| 20 October 2026 | Fair Work Commission [guidance note on generative AI](/policies/fwc-genai-guidance-note) commences |
| 10 December 2026 | [APP 1.7–1.9 transparency obligation](/policies/oaic-app-1-7-adm-transparency-guidance) applies |

From 20 October, under the [guidance note](https://www.fwc.gov.au/documents/resources/guidance-note-use-of-generative-ai-in-commission-cases.pdf), a person who uses generative AI to prepare a document for a Commission case must say so and how it was used, and state that they have checked it. That check "must be done by a person". Using generative AI only to check spelling or grammar, or to format, is excluded.

## Why it matters

In Policai's analysis, the OAIC's reading of "computer program" is most likely to catch organisations out. Its first worked example is a spreadsheet formula that ranks clients for health and aged care services, and the OAIC treats it as in scope. A review of only "AI" systems before 10 December may miss rule-based tools that do the same work. The OAIC advises entities in doubt to "take a cautious approach".

The [guidance note](https://www.fwc.gov.au/documents/resources/guidance-note-use-of-generative-ai-in-commission-cases.pdf) applies to anyone who uses generative AI to prepare a document lodged in a Commission case, not only lawyers (clause 6). Self-represented parties who draft with a chatbot will need the disclosure and the checking statement.

On data centres, the [PM&C consultation](/policies/pmc-ai-infrastructure-consultation-2026) closed on 9 October 2026. The [consultation page](https://www.pmc.gov.au/domestic-policy/office-ai/getting-it-right-building-ai-infrastructure-works-australia) says the Government will work with the states and territories on mandatory standards for how data centres use energy, water and land, and then plans to legislate them. The Senate report is due on 16 November.
