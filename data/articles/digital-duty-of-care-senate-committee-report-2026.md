The Senate Environment and Communications Legislation Committee tabled its final report on the [Online Safety Amendment (Digital Duty of Care) Bill 2026](/policies/federal-online-safety-amendment-digital-duty-of-care-bill-2026) on 20 November 2026 ([report](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/search/display/display.w3p;query=Id%3A%22committees%2Freportsen%2FRB000970%2F0000%22)). The bill was introduced in the Senate and its second reading moved on 16 November 2026; its status is "Before Senate" ([APH bill page](https://www.aph.gov.au/Parliamentary_Business/Bills_Legislation/Bills_Search_Results/Result?bId=r7553)).

## What it says

The majority makes four recommendations ([report, chapter 3](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/search/display/display.w3p;query=Id%3A%22committees%2Freportsen%2FRB000970%2F0005%22), paras 3.89 to 3.92):

1. amendments to establish "a civil right of action for individuals in respect of breaches of the Digital Duty of Care that cause harm or loss";
2. that the statutory review consider how well the duty addresses online hate and vilification based on protected characteristics;
3. an amendment confirming the bill's intention "to regulate digital communication platforms, such as social media platforms", and that it is not intended to apply to low-risk websites or online services such as small business websites, online marketplaces, booking platforms or religious websites focused on education or doctrinal instruction; and
4. that the Senate pass the bill, subject to consideration of recommendations 1 and 3.

The committee calls the bill "a substantial and welcome improvement to Australia's online safety regulatory framework" ([report, chapter 3](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/search/display/display.w3p;query=Id%3A%22committees%2Freportsen%2FRB000970%2F0005%22), para 3.88). Coalition senators recommend that the bill "not be passed" ([dissenting report](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/search/display/display.w3p;query=Id%3A%22committees%2Freportsen%2FRB000970%2F0006%22), para 1.135).

## Who it affects

The bill would place the duty on online services, including services that generate material using AI ([record](/policies/federal-online-safety-amendment-digital-duty-of-care-bill-2026)). AI came up in evidence. DIGI asked for clarity on how the duty applies to low-risk services such as "enterprise AI tools" ([report, chapter 2](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/search/display/display.w3p;query=Id%3A%22committees%2Freportsen%2FRB000970%2F0004%22), para 2.42). Dr Honni van Rijswijk suggested adding "manipulative artificial intelligence companions" to the harms to children ([report, chapter 2](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/search/display/display.w3p;query=Id%3A%22committees%2Freportsen%2FRB000970%2F0004%22), para 2.70). Richard Fleming, General Counsel at the Office of the eSafety Commissioner, said early criteria for the lowest-risk services include services "where there are no AI chatbots" ([report, chapter 2](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/search/display/display.w3p;query=Id%3A%22committees%2Freportsen%2FRB000970%2F0004%22), para 2.142).

Coalition senators note that the existing codes and standards include "safeguards against harmful content generated by AI chatbots", and that the bill abolishes all 17 of them after a 12-month transition ([dissenting report](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/search/display/display.w3p;query=Id%3A%22committees%2Freportsen%2FRB000970%2F0006%22), paras 1.28 and 1.71). Those include the [eSafety codes for generative AI services and AI companion chatbots](/policies/esafety-genai-industry-codes).

## Analysis

In Policai's analysis, the committee's view and recommendations do not address AI services. Recommendation 3 names digital communication platforms such as social media as the bill's target and lists low-risk services to exclude, but says nothing about generative AI or companion services. The eSafety evidence treats an AI chatbot as a sign that a service is not lowest risk. An amendment drafted to recommendation 3 would need to say where AI services sit.

In Policai's analysis, a civil right of action drafted against the duty itself would reach every service the duty covers, including AI services. Whatever the Senate decides, the [eSafety generative AI codes](/policies/esafety-genai-industry-codes) stay in force for now: the bill would repeal them only 12 months after Royal Assent ([record](/policies/esafety-genai-industry-codes)).
