The House of Representatives agreed to the third reading of the [Online Safety Amendment (Digital Duty of Care) Bill 2026](/policies/federal-online-safety-amendment-digital-duty-of-care-bill-2026) on 28 October 2026, after agreeing to 15 government and 8 crossbench amendments in consideration in detail ([APH bill page](https://www.aph.gov.au/Parliamentary_Business/Bills_Legislation/Bills_Search_Results/Result?bId=r7553)). The Senate Environment and Communications Legislation Committee is due to report on the bill on 20 November 2026 ([APH bill page](https://www.aph.gov.au/Parliamentary_Business/Bills_Legislation/Bills_Search_Results/Result?bId=r7553)).

## What it says

The passed text adds a new category to the list of material and conduct that is harmful to children in proposed section 25E ([third reading print, p 39](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/bills/r7553_third-reps/toc_pdf/26131b01.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf)):

- "(da) material or conduct that simulates emotional intimacy and encourages or fosters a child's dependence on it in place of human relationships".

It matches item 1 of the amendment sheet circulated by Ms Chaney ([Chaney amendments, item 1](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/amend/r7553_amend_94de72af-fd1a-4779-b6c7-c0230408dc0f/upload_pdf/26131%20Chaney.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf)).

A new section 27BA requires user empowerment tools to be accessible, easy to use, prominently available and functional on an ongoing basis, without impairing the service or user experience ([third reading print, pp 48–49](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/bills/r7553_third-reps/toc_pdf/26131b01.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf)). Breach carries a civil penalty of 6,000 penalty units ([third reading print, p 49](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/bills/r7553_third-reps/toc_pdf/26131b01.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf)).

The supplementary explanatory memorandum calls the government amendments "technical corrections and changes" ([supplementary EM, outline](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/ems/r7553_ems_2cf25142-dfcf-411d-ad6e-dc1b8d4c26d1/upload_pdf/JC019304.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf)). Three points stand out:

- **Fake nude apps.** Amendments 1 and 2 replace "and" with "or" in section 86B, so a removal notice may issue if an app or website was either designed for the predominant purpose of generating fake nude material or is predominantly used for that ([supplementary EM, amendments 1 and 2](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/ems/r7553_ems_2cf25142-dfcf-411d-ad6e-dc1b8d4c26d1/upload_pdf/JC019304.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf); [third reading print, p 5](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/bills/r7553_third-reps/toc_pdf/26131b01.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf)).
- **Industry codes and standards.** Amendments 3 and 4 provide that the validity of codes and standards registered before commencement "is not affected, and is taken never to have been affected" by specified failures to meet consultation requirements ([supplementary EM, amendments 3 and 4](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/ems/r7553_ems_2cf25142-dfcf-411d-ad6e-dc1b8d4c26d1/upload_pdf/JC019304.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf); [third reading print, p 28](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/bills/r7553_third-reps/toc_pdf/26131b01.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf)).
- **Excluded material.** Amendments 5 to 8 take excluded material and class 2 material out of seriously harmful material, and extend the entertainment exclusion to on-demand program services ([supplementary EM, amendments 5–8](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/ems/r7553_ems_2cf25142-dfcf-411d-ad6e-dc1b8d4c26d1/upload_pdf/JC019304.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf)).

Commencement is unchanged ([third reading print, p 2](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/bills/r7553_third-reps/toc_pdf/26131b01.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf)).

## Who it affects

The duty applies to services listed in proposed section 25B, including "a service that generates material using artificial intelligence and that uses an internet carriage service in the course of doing so" ([third reading print, p 36](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/bills/r7553_third-reps/toc_pdf/26131b01.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf)). Section 27BA applies to any service that provides user empowerment tools. The section 86B change affects app distribution and search engine services that receive removal notices. The validity amendments matter to providers bound by registered codes and standards, including the [eSafety generative AI codes](/policies/esafety-genai-industry-codes).

## Analysis

In Policai's analysis, paragraph 25E(1)(da) is the House's clearest change for AI products. It does not name AI, but material that "simulates emotional intimacy" and fosters a child's dependence "in place of human relationships" describes a risk most associated with AI companion and chatbot services, which paragraph 25B(1)(h) already brings within scope. As a harm-to-children item, it protects children rather than all users.

In Policai's analysis, the either-or test in section 86B lowers the bar for removal notices against nudify apps: the Commissioner no longer needs both the design purpose and the pattern of use.

The bill is not law, and the Senate could still change it. The APH status still reads "Before Reps" ([APH bill page](https://www.aph.gov.au/Parliamentary_Business/Bills_Legislation/Bills_Search_Results/Result?bId=r7553)).
