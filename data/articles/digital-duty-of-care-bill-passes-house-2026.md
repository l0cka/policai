The House of Representatives agreed to the third reading of the [Online Safety Amendment (Digital Duty of Care) Bill 2026](/policies/federal-online-safety-amendment-digital-duty-of-care-bill-2026) on 28 October 2026, after agreeing to 15 government and 8 crossbench amendments in consideration in detail ([APH bill page](https://www.aph.gov.au/Parliamentary_Business/Bills_Legislation/Bills_Search_Results/Result?bId=r7553)). The bill was introduced on 15 October 2026 and referred the same day to the Senate Environment and Communications Legislation Committee, which is due to report on 20 November 2026 ([APH bill page](https://www.aph.gov.au/Parliamentary_Business/Bills_Legislation/Bills_Search_Results/Result?bId=r7553)). Policai covered the bill as introduced in an earlier note. This note covers what changed in the House.

## What it says

The passed text adds a new category to the list of material and conduct that is harmful to children in proposed section 25E ([third reading print, p 39](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/bills/r7553_third-reps/toc_pdf/26131b01.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf)):

- "(da) material or conduct that simulates emotional intimacy and encourages or fosters a child's dependence on it in place of human relationships".

The same wording appears as the first item on the amendment sheet circulated by Ms Chaney ([Chaney amendments, item 1](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/amend/r7553_amend_94de72af-fd1a-4779-b6c7-c0230408dc0f/upload_pdf/26131%20Chaney.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf)). Under proposed section 25C, a safe online environment is one in which children in Australia are protected from material and conduct that is harmful to children ([third reading print, p 37](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/bills/r7553_third-reps/toc_pdf/26131b01.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf)).

A new section 27BA requires user empowerment tools to be designed, offered and managed so that each tool is accessible, easy to use, prominently available on the service, functional and available on an ongoing basis, and so that using it does not affect the general functionality of the service or reduce or impair user experience ([third reading print, pp 48–49](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/bills/r7553_third-reps/toc_pdf/26131b01.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf)). The civil penalty is 6,000 penalty units, the Commissioner may issue a formal warning, and the remedial direction power in section 27C extends to the new requirement ([third reading print, p 49](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/bills/r7553_third-reps/toc_pdf/26131b01.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf)). The Chaney sheet also adds section 27BA to the infringement notice, enforceable undertaking and injunction provisions ([Chaney amendments, items 5–8](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/amend/r7553_amend_94de72af-fd1a-4779-b6c7-c0230408dc0f/upload_pdf/26131%20Chaney.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf)).

The government amendments are described in the supplementary explanatory memorandum as making "technical corrections and changes" and providing "additional clarity for provisions that exclude material from the scope of harms" ([supplementary EM, outline](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/ems/r7553_ems_2cf25142-dfcf-411d-ad6e-dc1b8d4c26d1/upload_pdf/JC019304.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf)). Three points stand out:

- **Fake nude apps.** Amendments 1 and 2 replace "and" with "or" in section 86B, so the Commissioner may issue a removal notice to an app distribution service or search engine if an app or website either was designed for the predominant purpose of generating fake nude material or is predominantly used by end-users to generate it ([supplementary EM, amendments 1 and 2](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/ems/r7553_ems_2cf25142-dfcf-411d-ad6e-dc1b8d4c26d1/upload_pdf/JC019304.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf); [third reading print, p 5](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/bills/r7553_third-reps/toc_pdf/26131b01.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf)).
- **Industry codes and standards.** Amendments 3 and 4 provide that the validity of codes and standards registered before commencement "is not affected, and is taken never to have been affected" by specified failures to meet consultation requirements ([supplementary EM, amendments 3 and 4](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/ems/r7553_ems_2cf25142-dfcf-411d-ad6e-dc1b8d4c26d1/upload_pdf/JC019304.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf); [third reading print, p 28](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/bills/r7553_third-reps/toc_pdf/26131b01.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf)).
- **Excluded material.** Amendments 5 to 8 provide that seriously harmful material or conduct does not include excluded material or class 2 material, and add audiovisual content made available by an on-demand program service to the material excluded when produced for the sole or dominant purpose of entertaining ([supplementary EM, amendments 5–8](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/ems/r7553_ems_2cf25142-dfcf-411d-ad6e-dc1b8d4c26d1/upload_pdf/JC019304.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf)).

Commencement is unchanged in the passed text: Schedule 1 starts the day after Royal Assent, and Schedules 2 and 3 start the day after 12 months beginning on Royal Assent ([third reading print, p 2](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/bills/r7553_third-reps/toc_pdf/26131b01.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf)).

## Who it affects

The duty applies to online services listed in proposed section 25B, which include "a service that generates material using artificial intelligence and that uses an internet carriage service in the course of doing so" ([third reading print, p 36](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/bills/r7553_third-reps/toc_pdf/26131b01.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf)). The new paragraph 25E(1)(da) adds to what those services must protect children from, and section 27BA applies to any service that provides user empowerment tools ([third reading print, pp 39, 48–49](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/bills/r7553_third-reps/toc_pdf/26131b01.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf)). The section 86B change affects app distribution services and internet search engine services that receive removal notices, and the developers of apps that are mainly used to generate fake nude material ([third reading print, p 5](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/bills/r7553_third-reps/toc_pdf/26131b01.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf)). The validity amendments matter to industry bodies and providers bound by registered codes and standards, including those covering generative AI ([eSafety generative AI codes record](/policies/esafety-genai-industry-codes)).

## Analysis

In Policai's analysis, paragraph 25E(1)(da) is the House's clearest change for AI products. It does not name AI, but material that "simulates emotional intimacy" and fosters a child's dependence "in place of human relationships" describes a risk most closely associated with AI companion and chatbot services, which the bill already brings within scope through paragraph 25B(1)(h). It sits in the harmful-to-children list, not the seriously harmful list, so it concerns protecting children rather than all users.

In Policai's analysis, the either-or test in section 86B lowers the bar for removal notices against so-called nudify apps: the Commissioner no longer needs to be satisfied of both the design purpose and the pattern of use.

The bill is not law. The APH status still reads "Before Reps", the progress table lists no Senate stages yet, and the Senate committee report is due on 20 November 2026 ([APH bill page](https://www.aph.gov.au/Parliamentary_Business/Bills_Legislation/Bills_Search_Results/Result?bId=r7553)). The text described here could change in the Senate.
