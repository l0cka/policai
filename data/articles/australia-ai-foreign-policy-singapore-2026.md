Two statements on 24 November 2026 set out how the government intends to carry its AI policy abroad. Foreign Minister Penny Wong delivered a foreign policy statement to Parliament that names AI among five shifts in Australia's strategic environment ([statement](https://www.foreignminister.gov.au/minister/penny-wong/speech/securing-australias-future-changing-world-australian-foreign-policy-new-era)). The Prime Ministers of Australia and Singapore issued a joint statement on AI cooperation at the 11th Singapore-Australia Annual Leaders' Meeting ([joint statement](https://www.pm.gov.au/media/australia-singapore-cooperation-artificial-intelligence)).

## What it says

- **AI as a foreign policy shift.** Wong said "artificial intelligence and emerging technologies are spreading and disrupting faster than any previous revolutionary technologies", and that technology is changing "how wars are fought, how information is manipulated, how foreign actors can interfere in our societies" ([statement](https://www.foreignminister.gov.au/minister/penny-wong/speech/securing-australias-future-changing-world-australian-foreign-policy-new-era)).
- **Frontier AI.** Wong said Australia is "legislating mandatory safety and security protections for every frontier AI company that operates in Australia", and that it must ensure the race to develop frontier AI "does not reward speed over prudence" ([statement](https://www.foreignminister.gov.au/minister/penny-wong/speech/securing-australias-future-changing-world-australian-foreign-policy-new-era)). She said Australia is working with partners "on AI safety, making it a shared, global endeavour" ([statement](https://www.foreignminister.gov.au/minister/penny-wong/speech/securing-australias-future-changing-world-australian-foreign-policy-new-era)).
- **Testing and assurance with Singapore.** The leaders agreed to deepen cooperation "through exchanges on AI testing, evaluation and assurance, and workforce capability and skills development" ([joint statement](https://www.pm.gov.au/media/australia-singapore-cooperation-artificial-intelligence)). They reaffirmed the 2024 Memorandum of Understanding on Artificial Intelligence Cooperation ([joint statement](https://www.pm.gov.au/media/australia-singapore-cooperation-artificial-intelligence)).
- **Standards and the region.** The leaders committed to cooperate on international AI governance and standards, "including via platforms such as the International Network for Advanced AI Measurement, Evaluation and Science", and said the Indo-Pacific "should have a voice in shaping AI's development" ([joint statement](https://www.pm.gov.au/media/australia-singapore-cooperation-artificial-intelligence)). They also agreed to explore support for Pacific-led AI capabilities and cited Singapore's 2027 ASEAN Chair Year as a chance to advance cooperation on AI testing standards and methodologies ([joint statement](https://www.pm.gov.au/media/australia-singapore-cooperation-artificial-intelligence)).
- **Human control.** The leaders agreed on "ensuring that AI remains under human direction, oversight and control" ([joint statement](https://www.pm.gov.au/media/australia-singapore-cooperation-artificial-intelligence)).

## Who it affects

Neither statement imposes a duty on any business or agency. The joint statement records commitments between the two governments ([joint statement](https://www.pm.gov.au/media/australia-singapore-cooperation-artificial-intelligence)). The obligations Wong refers to for frontier AI companies would come from the frontier AI legislation she describes, not from her statement ([statement](https://www.foreignminister.gov.au/minister/penny-wong/speech/securing-australias-future-changing-world-australian-foreign-policy-new-era)).

## Analysis

In Policai's analysis, the statements put the "global norms" limb of the [National AI Plan](/policies/national-ai-plan-2025) to work. The plan's goal of keeping Australians safe includes global norms, and the plan includes establishing the Australian AI Safety Institute ([record](/policies/national-ai-plan-2025)). The Singapore commitments on testing, evaluation and assurance are the kind of technical work such an institute would carry, though the joint statement does not name it.

In Policai's analysis, the more consequential line is Wong's. It ties Australia's international AI safety posture to a domestic law that has not yet been enacted. Partners will read the frontier AI protections as the measure of that commitment, so their drafting and timing now carry foreign policy weight as well as regulatory weight.

In Policai's analysis, the joint statement is an agreement to cooperate, not a set of shared standards. A testing protocol, mutual recognition arrangement or new MoU arising from it would be a separate instrument for the register.
