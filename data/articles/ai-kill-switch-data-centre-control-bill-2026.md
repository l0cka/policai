Andrew Gee MP introduced the AI Kill Switch and Data Centre Control Bill 2026 in the House of Representatives on 7 September 2026, and the second reading was moved the same day ([Parliament of Australia](https://www.aph.gov.au/Parliamentary_Business/Bills_Legislation/Bills_Search_Results/Result?bId=r7537)). It is a private member's bill, its status is "Before Reps", and it is not law ([record](/policies/federal-ai-kill-switch-and-data-centre-control-bill-2026)). If passed, the whole Act would commence the day after Royal Assent ([bill as introduced, s 2](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/bills/r7537_first-reps/toc_pdf/26119b01.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf)).

## What it says

The explanatory memorandum says the bill has two objectives: keeping advanced AI systems "subject to effective human oversight and control", and making sure large-scale AI infrastructure is built in a way that protects communities, agricultural land, the environment, public health, natural resources and consumers ([explanatory memorandum, Outline](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/ems/r7537_ems_17631485-ac43-4e8a-8287-57db34d490d8/upload_pdf/26119b01%20EM%20Gee.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf)).

**Part 2, the kill switch.** It applies to a "covered technology": an AI system developed using computer power whose cost exceeds an amount prescribed by the rules, calculated at prevailing Australian cloud computing prices, and that meets any other requirements in the rules ([bill as introduced, s 7](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/bills/r7537_first-reps/toc_pdf/26119b01.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf)). The bill itself sets no amount. A "responsible provider" is a constitutional corporation that operates a covered technology and makes it available to others "through a programmatic interface, hosted service or other similar mechanism", unless it does so solely for personal, academic or other non-commercial purposes ([s 8](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/bills/r7537_first-reps/toc_pdf/26119b01.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf)).

- **Shutdown capability.** A responsible provider must "at all times" be able to stop inference, terminate or suspend user access, prevent use patterns that risk a critical incident or a breach of law, and shut the technology down. The civil penalty is $3,000,000, and each day is a separate contravention ([s 12](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/bills/r7537_first-reps/toc_pdf/26119b01.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf)). The Minister must publish voluntary technical standards for shutting down a covered technology within 12 months of commencement ([s 15](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/bills/r7537_first-reps/toc_pdf/26119b01.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf)).
- **Critical incidents.** These include sabotage of a lawful attempt to shut the technology down, unintended operation that causes death or injury or a prescribed amount of economic damage, the technology concealing a capability or action from monitoring, and a "loss-of-control scenario". Red-teaming and other structured testing in a controlled environment are excluded ([ss 9–11](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/bills/r7537_first-reps/toc_pdf/26119b01.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf)). A provider must notify the Minister within 24 hours of becoming aware of an incident and give a written report within 2 days ([s 16](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/bills/r7537_first-reps/toc_pdf/26119b01.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf)).
- **Emergency directions.** The Minister may direct a provider to act if satisfied that a critical incident has occurred, is occurring or is imminent, presents "a significant risk of serious harm or damage", and the direction is "reasonably necessary and proportionate". A direction may require shutdown and the preservation of model weights and telemetry ([s 17](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/bills/r7537_first-reps/toc_pdf/26119b01.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf)). Failing to comply carries a civil penalty of $30,000,000 ([s 18](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/bills/r7537_first-reps/toc_pdf/26119b01.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf)). A provider may seek reconsideration within 48 hours; a direction not decided within 5 days is taken to be confirmed ([s 19](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/bills/r7537_first-reps/toc_pdf/26119b01.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf)). The Minister must table a statement on each direction in both Houses within 15 sitting days ([s 20](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/bills/r7537_first-reps/toc_pdf/26119b01.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf)) and cannot delegate the power to give one ([s 33](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/bills/r7537_first-reps/toc_pdf/26119b01.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf)).

**Part 3, the data centre moratorium.** From commencement, a constitutional corporation must not construct a new AI data centre or upgrade an existing one until after the "moratorium end day". The civil penalty is $30,000,000 ([ss 22–23](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/bills/r7537_first-reps/toc_pdf/26119b01.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf)). An AI data centre is a centre on one site, or connected sites, in Australia, owned or operated by one corporation or a group of related bodies corporate, that is used to develop or operate AI systems "at scale" or that exceeds 20 megawatts and is built for 20 kilowatts or more per rack or for liquid cooling ([s 21](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/bills/r7537_first-reps/toc_pdf/26119b01.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf)).

The moratorium ends only when other Acts ensure seven things: productive agricultural land and water supplies are not put at risk by data centres' energy and water needs, data centres do not raise consumers' water or power bills, they do not harm the environment or public health, affected communities can approve or reject a data centre and its energy source, and the Commonwealth "reviews and approves AI systems before those systems are first released or supplied in Australia". An Act must also expressly end Part 3 ([s 24](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/bills/r7537_first-reps/toc_pdf/26119b01.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf)). The explanatory memorandum says this means "any decision to end the moratorium is subject to direct parliamentary consideration and approval" ([explanatory memorandum, cll 21–24](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/ems/r7537_ems_17631485-ac43-4e8a-8287-57db34d490d8/upload_pdf/26119b01%20EM%20Gee.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf)).

The bill would be enforced through the monitoring, investigation, civil penalty, infringement notice, enforceable undertaking and injunction provisions of the Regulatory Powers (Standard Provisions) Act 2014 ([ss 25–31](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/bills/r7537_first-reps/toc_pdf/26119b01.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf)). The explanatory memorandum says it will have no financial impact ([explanatory memorandum](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/ems/r7537_ems_17631485-ac43-4e8a-8287-57db34d490d8/upload_pdf/26119b01%20EM%20Gee.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf)).

## Who it affects

Part 2 would apply to corporations that offer AI systems above the compute-cost threshold to others through an interface or hosted service ([ss 7–8](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/bills/r7537_first-reps/toc_pdf/26119b01.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf)). Part 3 would apply to every constitutional corporation that would build or upgrade an AI data centre in Australia ([ss 21–23](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/bills/r7537_first-reps/toc_pdf/26119b01.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf)). The Act would bind the Crown in right of the Commonwealth, though the Crown could not be liable to a pecuniary penalty ([ss 4, 28(4)](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/bills/r7537_first-reps/toc_pdf/26119b01.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf)).

## Analysis

In Policai's analysis, the two parts work very differently. Part 2 depends on rules: until the Minister prescribes a compute-cost amount, no AI system meets the definition of a covered technology, so the shutdown, reporting and direction duties would have nothing to attach to. Part 3 needs no rules. It would apply from the day after Royal Assent, and "upgrading" is not defined, so the reach of the ban on existing sites would turn on how that word is read.

The moratorium's last condition goes beyond infrastructure. It cannot end until the Commonwealth has a scheme to review and approve AI systems before release, which Australia does not have. In Policai's analysis, the bill therefore links data centre construction to a national pre-market approval regime for AI.

The bill takes the opposite approach to current policy. The Government's [expectations for data centres and AI infrastructure](/policies/expectations-data-centres-ai-infrastructure-2026) are not law and work through prioritisation: energy-intensive proposals not closely aligned with them "will not be prioritised by Commonwealth regulatory assessments" ([expectations](https://www.industry.gov.au/publications/expectations-data-centres-and-ai-infrastructure-developers)). The bill would replace prioritisation with a prohibition. For now it is a private member's bill before the House, not an Act ([Parliament of Australia](https://www.aph.gov.au/Parliamentary_Business/Bills_Legislation/Bills_Search_Results/Result?bId=r7537)).
