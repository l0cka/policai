Andrew Gee MP introduced the AI Kill Switch and Data Centre Control Bill 2026 in the House of Representatives on 7 September 2026, and the second reading was moved the same day ([Parliament of Australia](https://www.aph.gov.au/Parliamentary_Business/Bills_Legislation/Bills_Search_Results/Result?bId=r7537)). It is a private member's bill, its status is "Before Reps", and it is not law ([record](/policies/federal-ai-kill-switch-and-data-centre-control-bill-2026)). If passed, the whole Act would commence the day after Royal Assent ([bill as introduced, s 2](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/bills/r7537_first-reps/toc_pdf/26119b01.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf)).

## What it says

**Part 2, the kill switch.** It applies to a "covered technology": an AI system developed using computer power whose cost exceeds an amount prescribed by the rules ([s 7](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/bills/r7537_first-reps/toc_pdf/26119b01.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf)).

- **Shutdown capability.** A responsible provider must "at all times" be able to stop inference, suspend user access and shut the technology down. The civil penalty is $3,000,000, and each day is a separate contravention ([s 12](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/bills/r7537_first-reps/toc_pdf/26119b01.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf)).
- **Critical incidents.** These include sabotage of a lawful shutdown attempt, unintended operation that causes death or injury, and a "loss-of-control scenario" ([ss 9–11](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/bills/r7537_first-reps/toc_pdf/26119b01.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf)). A provider must notify the Minister within 24 hours and give a written report within 2 days ([s 16](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/bills/r7537_first-reps/toc_pdf/26119b01.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf)).
- **Emergency directions.** The Minister may direct a provider to act, including to shut down, if a critical incident presents "a significant risk of serious harm or damage" ([s 17](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/bills/r7537_first-reps/toc_pdf/26119b01.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf)). Failing to comply carries a civil penalty of $30,000,000 ([s 18](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/bills/r7537_first-reps/toc_pdf/26119b01.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf)).

**Part 3, the data centre moratorium.** From commencement, a constitutional corporation must not construct a new AI data centre or upgrade an existing one, with a civil penalty of $30,000,000 ([ss 22–23](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/bills/r7537_first-reps/toc_pdf/26119b01.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf)). An AI data centre includes one that exceeds 20 megawatts and is built for 20 kilowatts or more per rack or for liquid cooling ([s 21](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/bills/r7537_first-reps/toc_pdf/26119b01.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf)).

The moratorium ends only when other Acts ensure seven things, including that data centres do not raise consumers' water or power bills, that affected communities can approve or reject a data centre, and that the Commonwealth "reviews and approves AI systems before those systems are first released or supplied in Australia". An Act must also expressly end Part 3 ([s 24](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/bills/r7537_first-reps/toc_pdf/26119b01.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf); [explanatory memorandum](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/ems/r7537_ems_17631485-ac43-4e8a-8287-57db34d490d8/upload_pdf/26119b01%20EM%20Gee.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf)).

## Who it affects

Part 2 would apply to constitutional corporations that make AI systems above the threshold available to others "through a programmatic interface, hosted service or other similar mechanism", other than solely for non-commercial purposes ([s 8](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/bills/r7537_first-reps/toc_pdf/26119b01.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf)). Part 3 would apply to every constitutional corporation that would build or upgrade an AI data centre in Australia ([ss 21–23](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/bills/r7537_first-reps/toc_pdf/26119b01.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf)).

## Analysis

In Policai's analysis, the two parts work very differently. Part 2 depends on rules: until the Minister prescribes a compute-cost amount, no AI system is a covered technology, so its duties would have nothing to attach to. Part 3 needs no rules and would apply from the day after Royal Assent. "Upgrading" is not defined, so the ban's reach over existing sites would turn on how that word is read.

The moratorium's last condition goes beyond infrastructure. It links data centre construction to a national pre-market approval regime for AI, which Australia does not have.

The bill also departs from current policy. The Government's [expectations for data centres and AI infrastructure](/policies/expectations-data-centres-ai-infrastructure-2026) are not law: proposals not closely aligned with them "will not be prioritised by Commonwealth regulatory assessments" ([expectations](https://www.industry.gov.au/publications/expectations-data-centres-and-ai-infrastructure-developers)). The bill would replace prioritisation with a prohibition.
