The Digital Transformation Agency (DTA) has published version 2.2 of the Standard for AI transparency statements. The standard's page was last updated on 20 November 2026 and links to a PDF titled "Standard for AI transparency statements version 2.2" ([DTA standard page](https://www.digital.gov.au/ai/ai-in-government-policy/standard-ai-transparency-statements)). The standard sets what agencies must publish under the [Policy for the Responsible Use of AI in Government](/policies/policy-responsible-use-ai-government-v2), and the PDF's cover says it supports version 2.1 of that policy ([version 2.2 PDF](https://www.digital.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/2026-11/Standard%20for%20AI%20transparency%20statements%20version%202.2.pdf)). Policai's record for the policy still lists version 2.1 of the standard ([record](/policies/policy-responsible-use-ai-government-v2)).

## What it says

The standard says transparency statements are meant to make it easy for the public to understand how agencies adopt and use AI, and that they "also provide a way for members of the public to report concerns or request information about an agency's use of AI" ([DTA standard page](https://www.digital.gov.au/ai/ai-in-government-policy/standard-ai-transparency-statements)).

Agencies must publish a statement on their public-facing website, and should link to it from the site's global menu, "consistent with the approach taken for privacy policies" ([DTA standard page](https://www.digital.gov.au/ai/ai-in-government-policy/standard-ai-transparency-statements)). They do not have to list individual use cases, but may publish an AI use case register ([DTA standard page](https://www.digital.gov.au/ai/ai-in-government-policy/standard-ai-transparency-statements)).

A statement must contain nine items of minimum information ([version 2.2 PDF](https://www.digital.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/2026-11/Standard%20for%20AI%20transparency%20statements%20version%202.2.pdf)):

- **Intentions.** The agency's intentions "behind using or not using AI".
- **Classification.** Current AI use classified by the usage patterns, and separately by the domains, in [Appendix D](https://www.digital.gov.au/ai/ai-in-government-policy/policy-responsible-use-ai-government-version-21-appendix-d-classification-system-ai-use) of the AI policy.
- **Public interaction.** Whether the agency's use includes instances where the public "may directly interact with AI or its outputs", with a brief overview where applicable.
- **Significant impact.** Whether there is "any usage where AI or its outputs may significantly impact the public", with a brief overview where applicable.
- **Monitoring and safeguards.** How the agency monitors and maintains AI systems through their lifecycle, and a description of "the safeguards the agency uses to protect staff and the public from negative impacts".
- **Compliance.** An overview of the agency's compliance with the AI policy.
- **Contact.** "Contact details, such as email and phone number, for members of the public to make enquiries about an agency's AI use and report AI safety concerns."

Statements must use plain language, must be reviewed and updated at least once a year, when the agency makes a significant change to its approach to AI, and "when any new factor materially impacts the statement's accuracy". A statement must clearly display when it was last updated, and agencies must notify the DTA each time they update it ([version 2.2 PDF](https://www.digital.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/2026-11/Standard%20for%20AI%20transparency%20statements%20version%202.2.pdf)).

## Who it affects

The standard applies to all non-corporate Commonwealth entities as defined by the Public Governance, Performance and Accountability Act 2013, excluding agencies in the defence portfolio and the national intelligence community ([DTA standard page](https://www.digital.gov.au/ai/ai-in-government-policy/standard-ai-transparency-statements)). Newly established agencies have three months from the date they are established to meet it ([DTA standard page](https://www.digital.gov.au/ai/ai-in-government-policy/standard-ai-transparency-statements)).

The standard sets no other commencement or transition date. It does not say by when an agency that already publishes a statement must bring it into line with version 2.2 ([version 2.2 PDF](https://www.digital.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/2026-11/Standard%20for%20AI%20transparency%20statements%20version%202.2.pdf)).

## Analysis

In Policai's analysis, the contact requirement is the part with the most practical effect. A statement must now name a way for the public to report AI safety concerns, not only to make enquiries, and gives a phone number as an example of the contact details expected. That makes each statement a point of contact as well as a disclosure.

In Policai's analysis, the significant-impact item asks a broad question. It asks whether there is any use where AI or its outputs may significantly impact the public, and the standard's wording does not exclude use where a person reviews the output before it reaches the public. Read with the Appendix D decision-making pattern, which covers AI that "substantially and directly" informs a decision affecting rights or interests ([Appendix D](https://www.digital.gov.au/ai/ai-in-government-policy/policy-responsible-use-ai-government-version-21-appendix-d-classification-system-ai-use)), an agency that uses AI to triage or score cases for human decision makers will need to consider whether it has something to describe here.

In Policai's analysis, the absence of a date for existing statements means statements on agency websites may follow different versions of the standard for up to a year. The standard's own review triggers are the annual review, a significant change in approach and a new factor that materially affects accuracy. It does not say whether a new version of the standard is such a factor.

In Policai's analysis, the timing overlaps with the Privacy Act's automated decision-making transparency obligation, which applies from 10 December 2026 and requires APP entities to describe certain computer-assisted decisions in their privacy policies (see the [OAIC guidance](/policies/oaic-app-1-7-adm-transparency-guidance)). Agencies covered by both will be describing related uses of AI in two public documents on different review cycles, and readers may compare the two.
