On 31 August 2026 the Attorney-General's Department opened a [consultation](/policies/agd-privacy-reform-exposure-draft-2026) on an Exposure Draft Privacy Amendment (Personal Data Protection) Bill 2026 and a consultation paper ([consultation hub](https://consultations.ag.gov.au/rights-and-protections/privacy-reform); [media release](https://ministers.ag.gov.au/media-centre/modernising-australias-privacy-laws-digital-age-31-08-2026)). Submissions closed on 18 September 2026 ([consultation hub](https://consultations.ag.gov.au/rights-and-protections/privacy-reform)). The department says the reforms will bolster privacy protections and help tackle emerging risks from new technologies, including artificial intelligence and wearable devices such as smart glasses, and that the Bill remains subject to further consideration by government ([consultation hub](https://consultations.ag.gov.au/rights-and-protections/privacy-reform)).

## What it says

The consultation hub publishes the [Exposure Draft Privacy Amendment (Personal Data Protection) Bill 2026](https://consultations.ag.gov.au/rights-and-protections/privacy-reform/user_uploads/exposure-draft-bill-2026.pdf) and a [Consultation Paper](https://consultations.ag.gov.au/rights-and-protections/privacy-reform/user_uploads/consultation_paper.pdf) ([consultation hub](https://consultations.ag.gov.au/rights-and-protections/privacy-reform)). Attorney-General Michelle Rowland's [media release](https://ministers.ag.gov.au/media-centre/modernising-australias-privacy-laws-digital-age-31-08-2026) of 31 August 2026 lists the key measures: a fair and reasonable test for collecting and using personal information; a right to erasure against large digital platforms such as social media and search; stronger standards for consent; and limits on trading personal information without clear permission.

The release says AI-powered tools and devices, such as those used in connected vehicles that continuously collect and process personal information, are amplifying privacy risks ([media release](https://ministers.ag.gov.au/media-centre/modernising-australias-privacy-laws-digital-age-31-08-2026)). The hub says the Bill was developed in response to feedback from the Privacy Act Review process and the 2024 reforms to the Privacy Act, with about 900 written submissions received across the process ([consultation hub](https://consultations.ag.gov.au/rights-and-protections/privacy-reform)).

The reforms build on the [Privacy and Other Legislation Amendment Act 2024](/policies/federal-privacy-and-other-legislation-amendment-act-2024) and follow the [Government Response to the Privacy Act Review Report](/policies/government-response-privacy-act-review-report) ([media release](https://ministers.ag.gov.au/media-centre/modernising-australias-privacy-laws-digital-age-31-08-2026); [register record](/policies/agd-privacy-reform-exposure-draft-2026)). The Bill had not been introduced to Parliament when the register record was staged ([register record](/policies/agd-privacy-reform-exposure-draft-2026)).

For how existing privacy law already applies to AI, and for the APP 1.7–1.9 transparency duty that applies from 10 December 2026 under the 2024 Act, see Policai's [privacy-and-AI explainer](/analysis/how-privacy-law-applies-to-ai) and [APP 1.7 note](/analysis/oaic-app-1-7-adm-transparency).

## Who it affects

The hub welcomed feedback from regulated entities, non-government organisations, industry, consumer organisations, legal experts, privacy advocates, academia and individuals ([consultation hub](https://consultations.ag.gov.au/rights-and-protections/privacy-reform)). The consultation is closed ([consultation hub](https://consultations.ag.gov.au/rights-and-protections/privacy-reform)). The department says the Bill remains subject to further consideration by government ([consultation hub](https://consultations.ag.gov.au/rights-and-protections/privacy-reform)).

## Analysis

In Policai's analysis, this note records a closed consultation on an exposure draft, not an introduced bill. The department states that the Bill remains subject to further consideration by government ([consultation hub](https://consultations.ag.gov.au/rights-and-protections/privacy-reform)).

In Policai's analysis, the AI framing is the department's and the minister's: the hub names artificial intelligence among emerging technology risks the draft is meant to tackle, and the media release cites AI-powered tools and devices as amplifying privacy risks ([consultation hub](https://consultations.ag.gov.au/rights-and-protections/privacy-reform); [media release](https://ministers.ag.gov.au/media-centre/modernising-australias-privacy-laws-digital-age-31-08-2026)). The draft sits beside the 2024 Act's APP 1.7–1.9 duty rather than replacing it; readers should use the existing notes for that duty rather than treat this consultation as the rule that already binds APP entities from 10 December 2026 ([register record](/policies/agd-privacy-reform-exposure-draft-2026); [privacy-and-AI explainer](/analysis/how-privacy-law-applies-to-ai); [APP 1.7 note](/analysis/oaic-app-1-7-adm-transparency)).

In Policai's analysis, the open question is what, if anything, the government introduces after the closed consultation. The hub cites about 900 written submissions across the Privacy Act Review and 2024 reforms as informing the draft; that page does not publish a post-consultation government response ([consultation hub](https://consultations.ag.gov.au/rights-and-protections/privacy-reform)).
