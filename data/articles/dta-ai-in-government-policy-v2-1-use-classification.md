The Digital Transformation Agency (DTA) published version 2.1 of the [Policy for the Responsible Use of AI in Government](/policies/policy-responsible-use-ai-government-v2) on 21 October 2026. The DTA describes the release as "minor updates" to version 2.0, which came into effect on 15 December 2025 ([DTA policy page](https://www.digital.gov.au/ai/ai-in-government-policy)). The live text includes Appendix D, a classification system for AI use that agencies must apply when they record, assess and publicly report their AI use cases ([Appendix D](https://www.digital.gov.au/ai/ai-in-government-policy/policy-responsible-use-ai-government-version-21-appendix-d-classification-system-ai-use)).

## What it says

Appendix D classifies AI use into seven usage patterns: decision-making and administrative action, analytics, strategic support, workplace productivity, workflow automation, image processing, and coding. It also sorts use into six domains: service delivery; compliance and fraud detection; law enforcement, intelligence and security; policy and legal; scientific; and corporate and enabling ([Appendix D](https://www.digital.gov.au/ai/ai-in-government-policy/policy-responsible-use-ai-government-version-21-appendix-d-classification-system-ai-use)).

Appendix D defines decision-making and administrative action as use of AI "to make, or substantially and directly inform, a decision or administrative action that could affect the rights or interests of an individual or entity", and adds that "this includes refusing or failing to take such an action". Its examples go beyond final decisions to "risk scoring, prioritisation recommendations, case triage, benefit assessment support and recommended enforcement actions for human decision makers" ([Appendix D](https://www.digital.gov.au/ai/ai-in-government-policy/policy-responsible-use-ai-government-version-21-appendix-d-classification-system-ai-use)). By contrast, workflow automation "executes or coordinates predefined processes, tasks or actions with limited discretion" ([Appendix D](https://www.digital.gov.au/ai/ai-in-government-policy/policy-responsible-use-ai-government-version-21-appendix-d-classification-system-ai-use)).

Agencies must apply the classification in three places: in their use case register, as the [Standard for accountability](https://www.digital.gov.au/ai/ai-in-government-policy/accountability) requires; when they assess a use case with the AI impact assessment tool; and in their AI transparency statement, as the [Standard for AI transparency statements](https://www.digital.gov.au/ai/ai-in-government-policy/standard-ai-transparency-statements) requires ([Appendix D](https://www.digital.gov.au/ai/ai-in-government-policy/policy-responsible-use-ai-government-version-21-appendix-d-classification-system-ai-use)).

## Who it affects

The policy is mandatory for non-corporate Commonwealth entities and encouraged for corporate Commonwealth entities; the Defence portfolio and the National Intelligence Community are listed as exceptions ([version 2.1 PDF](https://www.digital.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/2026-10/Policy%20for%20the%20responsible%20use%20of%20AI%20in%20government%202_1_1.pdf)).

For existing use cases not yet assessed, agencies "must determine whether they are in scope of this policy and apply all relevant policy actions by 30 April 2027" ([AI use case impact assessment](https://www.digital.gov.au/ai/ai-in-government-policy/ai-use-case-impact-assessment)). Where an impact assessment gives an in-scope use case an inherent high-risk rating, the agency must report it to its accountable official with the reasons for that rating. Once the agency has decided to deploy the use case, it must also report it to the DTA through the accountable official ([AI use case impact assessment](https://www.digital.gov.au/ai/ai-in-government-policy/ai-use-case-impact-assessment)).

## Analysis

In Policai's analysis, the decision-making pattern is the part to watch. By counting triage, risk scoring and recommendations to human decision makers as decision-making, Appendix D puts assistive tools in the same reporting category as systems that decide outright. An agency cannot keep a triage model out of that category simply because a person signs off the final decision.

In Policai's analysis, the definition sits close to the Privacy Act transparency test that applies from 10 December 2026. [APP 1.7](/policies/oaic-app-1-7-adm-transparency-guidance) reaches computer programs that make a decision, or do a thing substantially and directly related to making one, that could reasonably be expected to significantly affect an individual's rights or interests, where personal information is used ([register record](/policies/oaic-app-1-7-adm-transparency-guidance)). The tests are worded differently, but a use classified as decision-making under Appendix D may also raise the APP 1.7 question.
