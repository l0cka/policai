The Australian Government introduced the Online Safety Amendment (Digital Duty of Care) Bill 2026 in the House of Representatives on 15 October 2026, and the second reading was moved the same day ([Parliament of Australia](https://www.aph.gov.au/Parliamentary_Business/Bills_Legislation/Bills_Search_Results/Result?bId=r7553)). Its status is "Before Reps". It is a proposed law, not an Act.

## What it says

Schedule 3 of the bill is headed "Repeal of online content scheme industry codes and standards" ([bill as introduced, p 84](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/bills/r7553_first-reps/toc_word/26131b01.docx)). Its item 5 repeals Division 7 of Part 9 of the Online Safety Act 2021 ([bill as introduced, Sch 3 item 5](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/bills/r7553_first-reps/toc_word/26131b01.docx)). Division 7, sections 132 to 150, is the part of the Act that provides for industry codes and industry standards ([Online Safety Act 2021](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2021A00076/2026-09-12/2026-09-12/text/original/pdf)). The explanatory memorandum says of item 5: "This item repeals the Division" ([explanatory memorandum, p 104](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/ems/r7553_ems_646770dc-4740-4989-9e2e-e1e129e42251/upload_pdf/JC019225.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf)).

Schedules 2 and 3 would commence on "the day after the end of the period of 12 months beginning on the day this Act receives the Royal Assent" ([bill as introduced, s 2(1)](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/bills/r7553_first-reps/toc_word/26131b01.docx)). Schedule 1 would commence the day after Royal Assent ([bill as introduced, s 2(1)](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/bills/r7553_first-reps/toc_word/26131b01.docx)). The explanatory memorandum says the codes "will remain in effect during the 12 month period of transition" ([explanatory memorandum, pp 44–45](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/ems/r7553_ems_646770dc-4740-4989-9e2e-e1e129e42251/upload_pdf/JC019225.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf)). The second-reading speech said: "If the digital duty of care passes through parliament and royal assent, a 12-month transitional period will be in place before the digital duty of care comes into effect" ([Parliament of Australia](https://www.aph.gov.au/Parliamentary_Business/Bills_Legislation/Bills_Search_Results/Result?bId=r7553)).

Schedule 1 would also change the codes regime during that transition. Items 84 and 85 would raise the maximum civil penalties in sections 143(2) and 146(1) to 60,000 penalty units ([bill as introduced, p 25](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/bills/r7553_first-reps/toc_word/26131b01.docx)). Items 90 and 91 would add sections 140(5) and 145(4), which provide that existing codes and standards are not invalid because a consultation period was too short ([bill as introduced, p 28](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/bills/r7553_first-reps/toc_word/26131b01.docx)).

## Who it affects

Industry codes are registered under section 140 of the Act ([eSafety regulatory guidance, p 6](https://www.esafety.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-12/OnlineSafetyCodesandStandardsRegulatoryGuidanceDec2025.pdf)), and industry standards are determined under section 145. The 2024 standard for designated internet services was determined under section 145 and defines "high impact generative AI DIS" ([Federal Register of Legislation, F2024L00710](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2024L00710/asmade/2024-06-21/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_1/document_1.html)).

The register records that the eSafety Commissioner registered further codes in September 2025 that cover generative AI services and AI companion chatbots, and that they took effect on 9 March 2026 ([record](/policies/esafety-genai-industry-codes)). Those codes name Part 9, Division 7 of the Act as their basis ([Phase 2 Head Terms, cl 7.2(a)](https://www.esafety.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-09/Consolidated-Industry-Codes-of-Practice-for-the-Online-Industry-%28Class-1C-and-Class-2-Material%29-Head-Terms-9-September-2025.pdf)). The register records that in March 2026 eSafety reported on the risks AI companions pose to children, after issuing transparency notices to four providers in October 2025 ([record](/policies/esafety-genai-industry-codes)).

The bill was referred on 15 October 2026 to the Senate Environment and Communications Legislation Committee, with a report due on 20 November 2026 ([Parliament of Australia](https://www.aph.gov.au/Parliamentary_Business/Bills_Legislation/Bills_Search_Results/Result?bId=r7553)).

## Analysis

In Policai's analysis, repealing Division 7 would end every code and standard made under it, not only the AI rules. That includes the 2024 standards and both phases of codes. Generative AI services would move from specific rules to a general duty of care. Until the repeal starts, the codes and standards still apply, and the bill would raise their maximum penalties in the meantime. Whether the new duty is stricter or looser will depend on how it is written and enforced. The committee's report is the next public step.
