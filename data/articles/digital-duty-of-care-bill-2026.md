The Australian Government has introduced the Online Safety Amendment (Digital Duty of Care) Bill 2026 in the House of Representatives. The Parliament records that it was introduced and read a first time on 15 October 2026, and that the second reading was moved the same day ([Parliament of Australia](https://www.aph.gov.au/Parliamentary_Business/Bills_Legislation/Bills_Search_Results/Result?bId=r7553)). Its status is "Before Reps". It is a proposed law, not an Act.

## What it says

The register records that Schedule 3 of the bill would repeal the online-safety industry codes and standards 12 months after Royal Assent ([record](/policies/esafety-genai-industry-codes)). Schedule 3 is titled "Repeal of online content scheme industry codes and standards" ([Parliament of Australia](https://www.aph.gov.au/Parliamentary_Business/Bills_Legislation/Bills_Search_Results/Result?bId=r7553)).

The second-reading speech on 15 October 2026 said: "If the digital duty of care passes through parliament and royal assent, a 12-month transitional period will be in place before the digital duty of care comes into effect" ([Hansard, via OpenAustralia](https://www.openaustralia.org.au/debates/?id=2026-10-15.6.2)).

The codes it would repeal include those for generative AI services and AI companion chatbots. The register records that the eSafety Commissioner registered them in September 2025 under the Online Safety Act, and that they took effect on 9 March 2026. They impose age-assurance and safety obligations, with civil penalties of up to $49.5 million ([record](/policies/esafety-genai-industry-codes)).

The register records that the repeal has not occurred. Those codes stay in force ([record](/policies/esafety-genai-industry-codes)).

## Who it affects

The codes now apply to providers of generative AI services and AI companion chatbots, among other online services ([record](/policies/esafety-genai-industry-codes)). The register records that in March 2026 eSafety reported on the risks AI companions pose to children, after issuing transparency notices to four providers in October 2025 ([record](/policies/esafety-genai-industry-codes)).

The bill is a government bill in the Infrastructure, Transport, Regional Development, Communications, Sport and the Arts portfolio. It was referred on 15 October 2026 to the Senate Environment and Communications Legislation Committee, with a report due on 20 November 2026 ([Parliament of Australia](https://www.aph.gov.au/Parliamentary_Business/Bills_Legislation/Bills_Search_Results/Result?bId=r7553)). No amendments had been circulated when Policai checked the bill page.

## Analysis

In Policai's analysis, the bill would change how generative AI services are regulated for online safety. Today they follow specific registered codes. Under the bill they would answer to a general duty of care. Whether that is stricter or looser depends on how the duty is written and enforced, and the committee's report is the next public step. Until the bill passes and the 12 months run, the codes are the rules that apply. The register classes the codes as a regulation and their status as active.
