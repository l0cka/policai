On 4 November 2026 the Department of Education published [*A Digital Detox*](https://www.education.gov.au/schooling/resources/digital-detox), recording decisions of the Education Ministers Meeting on 2 November 2026 ([announcement](https://www.education.gov.au/schooling/announcements/digital-detox-rebalancing-technology-use-australian-schools); [PDF](https://www.education.gov.au/download/20853/digital-detox/46397/document/pdf)). The announcement says ministers agreed to reduce screen use, restrict the use of AI, and establish the Australian Schools Digital Detox Taskforce.

Policai records the ministers' decisions as [A Digital Detox](/policies/education-ministers-digital-detox-2026) and the related [Australian Framework for Generative Artificial Intelligence (AI) in Schools](/policies/australian-framework-generative-ai-in-schools).

## What it says

Ministers agreed to restrict student use of generative AI (GenAI) applications in primary schools, recognising this may involve continued use of some AI features built into technologies already in use, with timing, scope and application set by each jurisdiction or sector ([PDF](https://www.education.gov.au/download/20853/digital-detox/46397/document/pdf)).

They agreed to restrict student use of GenAI in secondary school assessments on the same jurisdictional basis ([PDF](https://www.education.gov.au/download/20853/digital-detox/46397/document/pdf)).

They also agreed to introduce, trial or consider screen-use limits in schools, and to establish the Australian Schools Digital Detox Taskforce to collect evidence, consult students, teachers and parents, and advise on further action ([PDF](https://www.education.gov.au/download/20853/digital-detox/46397/document/pdf)). The paper says the taskforce will give preliminary advice by mid-2027 and finish by the end of 2027 unless ministers decide otherwise ([PDF](https://www.education.gov.au/download/20853/digital-detox/46397/document/pdf)).

The announcement says the paper is not about removing technology from schools: students need digital skills, including using AI safely and responsibly, but technology must support learning rather than replace the thinking through which young people build knowledge and skills ([announcement](https://www.education.gov.au/schooling/announcements/digital-detox-rebalancing-technology-use-australian-schools)).

The paper recaps the [Australian Framework for Generative Artificial Intelligence (AI) in Schools](https://www.education.gov.au/schooling/resources/australian-framework-generative-artificial-intelligence-ai-schools). That page says the Framework guides responsible and ethical use of generative AI for people connected with school education, and that in June 2025 Education Ministers endorsed the 2024 Framework Review ([Framework page](https://www.education.gov.au/schooling/resources/australian-framework-generative-artificial-intelligence-ai-schools)).

Federal Education Minister Jason Clare's [5 November 2026 media release](https://ministers.education.gov.au/clare/digital-detox-schools-curriculum-reform-and-establishment-teaching-and-learning-commission) restates the GenAI restrictions and the new taskforce.

## Who it affects

Implementation sits with each jurisdiction or sector ([PDF](https://www.education.gov.au/download/20853/digital-detox/46397/document/pdf)). The Framework page addresses school leaders, teachers, support staff, service providers, parents, guardians, students and policy makers ([Framework page](https://www.education.gov.au/schooling/resources/australian-framework-generative-artificial-intelligence-ai-schools)).

In NSW, [NESA's Stage 6 take-home assessment and generative AI rules](/policies/nsw-nesa-stage-6-take-home-assessment-ai-rules) already cap take-home tasks and require marks to reflect students' own work rather than generative AI use ([NESA notice](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/news/all/new-take-home-assessment-rules); earlier note: [NESA's take-home assessment limit and generative AI rules for the HSC](/analysis/nesa-take-home-assessment-generative-ai-rules-2026)). How NSW applies the new ministerial GenAI assessment restriction beside those ACE Rules is for the jurisdiction to say.

## Analysis

In Policai's analysis, these GenAI decisions are a national policy agreement, not a Commonwealth statute or a single binding school rule. Force for students and schools arrives when each jurisdiction or sector sets timing, scope and application ([PDF](https://www.education.gov.au/download/20853/digital-detox/46397/document/pdf)).

In Policai's analysis, the open questions are how each system will define "secondary school assessments", how built-in AI features in primary technologies will be treated, and how the taskforce's mid-2027 advice will sit beside the Framework ([Digital Detox record](/policies/education-ministers-digital-detox-2026); [Framework record](/policies/australian-framework-generative-ai-in-schools)).
