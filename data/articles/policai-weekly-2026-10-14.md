On 12 October 2026 the Joint Select Committee on Artificial Intelligence wrote to the Prime Minister with early recommendations, including that the Government require frontier labs to monitor and report incidents, mandate independent evaluators embedded in those labs, and require early access to new models for agencies such as the Australian Signals Directorate ([committee letter](https://static.aph.gov.au/-/media/Committees/ai_ctte/JSC_AI_letter_20261012_Final.pdf)). This briefing covers the seven days to 14 October 2026. No development detected in that window is verified in Policai's feed yet, so each item was checked at its official source.

## What changed

- **Ministerial speech on frontier AI.** On 8 October the Assistant Minister for Science, Technology and the Digital Economy, Dr Andrew Charlton, said the Government has "committed to legislating National AI Standards for frontier AI" ([speech](https://www.minister.industry.gov.au/charlton/media/speech-the-sydney-trust-and-safety-festival)). He described systems regulation as placing "the onus on companies to build and run a rigorous process for finding, testing, reporting on and managing the risks of their systems".
- **Tasmanian expectations for AI data centres.** On 14 October the Premier announced a Local Community Benefit Fund that major AI and data-centre proponents will be expected to contribute to ([media release](https://www.premier.tas.gov.au/latest-news/2026/october/major-ai-investment-must-deliver-for-tasmanian-communities)). They will also be expected to meet their own infrastructure and network costs and publicly report their water and energy use each quarter.
- **Deepfake labels in elections.** At the committee's 14 October hearing, Electoral Commissioner Jeff Pope said one option in an Australian Electoral Commission consultation is that deepfakes in political communication carry a simple label, with the onus on the person authorising it. "There has been no decision made," he said ([proof Hansard](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/search/display/display.w3p;db=COMMITTEES;id=committees%2Fcommjnt%2F29981%2F0002;query=Id%3A%22committees%2Fcommjnt%2F29981%2F0000%22)).
- **South Australian Royal Commission into AI.** The Department of the Premier and Cabinet announced on 8 October that Dr Iain Ross AO was appointed Chair on 1 October, alongside Commissioners Kate Pounder and Professor Christopher Manning ([announcement](https://www.dpc.sa.gov.au/news/royal-commission-into-artificial-intelligence-announced)).
- **INSLM scoping of AI law for intelligence agencies.** The Independent National Security Legislation Monitor's annual report, published on 8 October, finds that the national intelligence community "has significant exemptions from most relevant existing laws that apply to the use of AI by government agencies" ([report, p 34](https://www.inslm.gov.au/system/files/2026-10/INSLM-annual-report-2025-26.pdf)). This is initial scoping, not a full review (p 28).

## Why it matters

In Policai's analysis, the committee's letter and the minister's speech point the same way: duties on frontier developers to find, test and report risks, rather than prescriptive technical rules. The obligations depend on legislation not yet released.

The committee also recommends national data centre standards implemented through state and territory planning laws. Tasmania has set its own expectations, and the [PM&C consultation](/policies/pmc-ai-infrastructure-consultation-2026), which closed on 9 October, will inform AI standards the Government plans to develop with the states and territories.
