The Online Safety and Other Legislation Amendment (My Face, My Rights) Bill 2025 is a private senator's bill sponsored by Senator David Pocock ([Parliament of Australia](https://www.aph.gov.au/Parliamentary_Business/Bills_Legislation/Bills_Search_Results/Result?bId=s1471)). It was introduced in the Senate on 24 November 2025, debated at second reading on 25 June 2026, and remains "Before Senate" ([record](/policies/federal-online-safety-other-legislation-amendment-my-face-my-rights-bill-2025)). It is a proposed law, not an Act.

## What it says

Schedule 1 would add a deepfake removal scheme to the Online Safety Act 2021. Schedule 2 would add to the Privacy Act 1988 a cause of action for the wrongful use or disclosure of deepfake material ([explanatory memorandum](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/ems/s1471_ems_6b78644a-3a08-4850-911f-b9f9d6ade06e/upload_pdf/EM_25S29.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf)). Both would commence the day after Royal Assent ([bill as introduced, s 2](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/bills/s1471_first-senate/toc_pdf/25S2920.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf)).

Deepfake material is an image, video or audio that depicts "in a realistic way, an individual's face or voice", where the individual is an Australian adult or Australian child, and the depiction "is false on account of the material being created or altered entirely, substantially or in a significant respect using technology" ([bill as introduced, s 21A(1)](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/bills/s1471_first-senate/toc_pdf/25S2920.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf)). Cyber-bullying, cyber-abuse, intimate image and abhorrent violent material stay under their existing parts of the Act (s 21A(2)).

Under the Online Safety Act changes ([bill as introduced, Sch 1](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/bills/s1471_first-senate/toc_pdf/25S2920.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf)):

- An end-user ordinarily resident in Australia who posts deepfake material knowing it is non-consensual would face a civil penalty of 500 penalty units (s 93B).
- A subject aged 16 or over, or a parent, guardian or authorised person, could complain to the eSafety Commissioner (ss 37A, 37B).
- The Commissioner could require a service, end-user or host to remove the material within 24 hours, with a 500 penalty unit penalty for failing to comply (ss 93D–93G).

The Privacy Act cause of action would arise where a person uses or discloses deepfake material of an individual without consent, knowing or reckless that it was made using technology, and the individual suffers detriment or the person makes a profit. It would be "actionable without proof of damage" ([bill as introduced, Sch 2, cl 7](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/bills/s1471_first-senate/toc_pdf/25S2920.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf)). Courts could award damages and other remedies, but not aggravated damages (cll 12, 13). Claims would generally have to start within 1 year of awareness or 3 years of the conduct, whichever is earlier, or before 21 for a plaintiff who was a minor (cl 15).

## Who it affects

The scheme would reach social media, relevant electronic and designated internet services, hosting providers and end-users ([bill as introduced, s 93A](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/bills/s1471_first-senate/toc_pdf/25S2920.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf)). Journalists working under professional standards, government agencies acting in good faith, law enforcement and intelligence agencies, and people under 18 would be exempt from the cause of action (Sch 2, cll 16–21). eSafety and the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner would implement it within existing resources ([explanatory memorandum](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/ems/s1471_ems_6b78644a-3a08-4850-911f-b9f9d6ade06e/upload_pdf/EM_25S29.pdf;fileType=application%2Fpdf)).

## Analysis

In Policai's analysis, the bill takes two routes to the same harm. The eSafety route is fast and turns on posting without consent. The court route reaches any use or disclosure, and its profit limb means a commercial use of a person's synthetic face or voice, such as a cloned voice in an advertisement, could ground a claim without any loss to the person depicted.

The definition turns on what the material shows, not the tool used, so the bill does not depend on a definition of AI.
