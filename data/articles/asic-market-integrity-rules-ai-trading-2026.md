The Australian Securities and Investments Commission (ASIC) has amended its market integrity rules for securities and futures markets to set specific obligations for trading algorithms, including those enabled by artificial intelligence (AI) and machine learning ([ASIC media release](https://www.asic.gov.au/about-asic/news-centre/find-a-media-release/2026-releases/26-226mr-asic-strengthens-ai-trading-safeguards-and-streamlines-market-integrity-rules)). The two amendment instruments were made under subsection 798G(1) of the Corporations Act 2001 and registered on 17 September 2026 ([record](/policies/asic-market-integrity-rules-ai-trading-2026)).

## What it says

The securities instrument defines a Trading Algorithm as a computer algorithm that automatically determines one or more order parameters "with limited or no human intervention" ([item 33](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2026L01223/asmade/2026-09-17/text/original/pdf)). Under the amended rules, a trading participant must:

- **Govern algorithms:** have controls and governance arrangements, including written procedures, for the development, testing, approval, deployment and monitoring of every trading algorithm it uses or offers to clients ([r 5.6.3B(1)](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2026L01223/asmade/2026-09-17/text/original/pdf)).
- **Test before use:** take reasonable steps to test that an algorithm will not interfere with market integrity before first use, before offering it to a client and before a material change ([r 5.6.3B(2)](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2026L01223/asmade/2026-09-17/text/original/pdf)).
- **Stop an algorithm:** have controls that enable immediate suspension, limitation or prohibition of the operation of one or more trading algorithms ([r 5.6.3(1)(c)(ii)](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2026L01223/asmade/2026-09-17/text/original/pdf)). ASIC's explanatory statement calls this the "kill switch" ([ES para 52](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2026L01223/asmade/2026-09-17/es/original/pdf)).
- **Monitor and surveil:** have monitoring that can immediately identify disruptive trading messages, and post-trade surveillance for false or misleading trading ([r 5.6.3A](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2026L01223/asmade/2026-09-17/text/original/pdf)).
- **Keep records:** keep records of algorithm governance and testing, and of flagged messages, for seven years ([rr 5.6.3A(3), 5.6.3B(4)](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2026L01223/asmade/2026-09-17/text/original/pdf)).

The false or misleading rule now also applies where a participant ought reasonably suspect that an order "will have the effect of creating" a false or misleading appearance ([r 5.7.1(2)(c)(ii)](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2026L01223/asmade/2026-09-17/text/original/pdf)). ASIC says the change is meant to cover orders generated by agentic AI systems that have that effect, "whether or not a person intended that effect" ([ES para 29](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2026L01223/asmade/2026-09-17/es/original/pdf)). The futures instrument inserts matching obligations in Part 2.2B ([F2026L01224](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2026L01224/asmade/2026-09-17/text/original/pdf)).

Each instrument commences 18 months after registration ([s 2](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2026L01223/asmade/2026-09-17/text/original/pdf)). The register records a Federal Register start date of 17 March 2028 for the securities amendments, while ASIC gives 18 March 2028 ([record](/policies/asic-market-integrity-rules-ai-trading-2026); [CS 63](https://www.asic.gov.au/regulatory-resources/find-a-document/consultations/cs-63-proposed-updates-to-rg-265-and-rg-266-and-withdrawal-of-rg-241)). ASIC is consulting on updated guidance in RG 265 and RG 266, and on withdrawing RG 241, until 5pm AEDT on 5 November 2026 ([CS 63](https://www.asic.gov.au/regulatory-resources/find-a-document/consultations/cs-63-proposed-updates-to-rg-265-and-rg-266-and-withdrawal-of-rg-241)).

## Who it affects

The rules bind trading participants of Australian securities and futures markets. ASIC counts 58 securities market participants, from large investment banks to small stockbrokers and principal traders ([ES para 8](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2026L01223/asmade/2026-09-17/es/original/pdf)). The obligations apply to manually entered and automated orders alike ([ASIC media release](https://www.asic.gov.au/about-asic/news-centre/find-a-media-release/2026-releases/26-226mr-asic-strengthens-ai-trading-safeguards-and-streamlines-market-integrity-rules)).

## Analysis

In Policai's analysis, this is binding AI-relevant regulation made through a sector regulator's existing rule-making power, not new AI legislation. Testing before deployment, a stop control for each algorithm and seven-year records are concrete duties, where most Australian AI governance material is guidance.

In Policai's analysis, the effect-based limb is the most significant change for AI. It moves the question from what a trader intended to what an order did. How ASIC will apply "ought reasonably suspect" to an agentic system's orders is not yet settled. The draft guidance in CS 63 is the place to raise it before 5 November.
