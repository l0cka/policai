Victoria's Minister for Economic Growth and Jobs, Danny Pearson, launched the government's [AI Mission Statement](/policies/victorian-ai-mission-statement) on 30 January 2026, describing it as "a bold vision for Victoria to become a global leader in responsible and ethical adoption of AI" ([Premier of Victoria](https://www.premier.vic.gov.au/victorias-ai-future-driving-jobs-opportunity-and-growth)). The Department of Jobs, Skills, Industry and Regions says the statement "sets out the Victorian Government's plan to responsibly develop and adopt artificial intelligence" ([DJSIR](https://djsir.vic.gov.au/priorities-and-initiatives/ai-mission-statement)). The register holds it as an active Victorian framework ([record](/policies/victorian-ai-mission-statement)).

## What it says

The statement is titled "Victoria: AI-Driven, Business-Ready" and rests on six pillars: investment attraction and adoption; data centres and digital infrastructure; local innovation, products and services; talent and workforce; ethical AI use; and public sector adoption ([AI Mission Statement](https://djsir.vic.gov.au/__data/assets/word_doc/0006/2456601/AI-Mission-Statement-Accessible-Version.docx)). It adopts the OECD definition of an AI system ([AI Mission Statement](https://djsir.vic.gov.au/__data/assets/word_doc/0006/2456601/AI-Mission-Statement-Accessible-Version.docx)).

Its economic case is that AI "could generate up to $30 billion in Gross State Product for Victoria over the next 10 years", and it notes that Melbourne's CBD is home to 188 AI companies and around 22% of all clustered AI firms in Australia ([AI Mission Statement](https://djsir.vic.gov.au/__data/assets/word_doc/0006/2456601/AI-Mission-Statement-Accessible-Version.docx)). The spending it lists includes $5.5 million for a Sustainable Data Centre Action Plan, a $150 million Victorian Investment Fund that can support AI investment, $10 million for the Cremorne Digital Hub and $10 million for the Australian Centre for Artificial Intelligence in Medical Innovation ([AI Mission Statement](https://djsir.vic.gov.au/__data/assets/word_doc/0006/2456601/AI-Mission-Statement-Accessible-Version.docx)). It puts the Digital Jobs – AI Career Conversion program at $8.2 million, as does the department's page; the Premier's launch release gives $8.1 million ([AI Mission Statement](https://djsir.vic.gov.au/__data/assets/word_doc/0006/2456601/AI-Mission-Statement-Accessible-Version.docx); [DJSIR](https://djsir.vic.gov.au/priorities-and-initiatives/ai-mission-statement); [Premier of Victoria](https://www.premier.vic.gov.au/victorias-ai-future-driving-jobs-opportunity-and-growth)).

The "Ethical AI use" pillar says Victorians "need to know that AI is being used by government and businesses in ways that are transparent, accountable and aligned with their values", and warns that "any safeguards must be balanced. Too much red tape risks slowing the development of local AI solutions and discouraging business uptake" ([AI Mission Statement](https://djsir.vic.gov.au/__data/assets/word_doc/0006/2456601/AI-Mission-Statement-Accessible-Version.docx)). It says national copyright and industrial relations frameworks "remain a key tool" for intellectual property and worker protection but that AI "will stretch those frameworks' limits", and that coordinated action between Victoria, the other states and territories and the Commonwealth "will be key to addressing privacy, IP, and worker challenges" ([AI Mission Statement](https://djsir.vic.gov.au/__data/assets/word_doc/0006/2456601/AI-Mission-Statement-Accessible-Version.docx)). Its actions under this pillar are to ([AI Mission Statement](https://djsir.vic.gov.au/__data/assets/word_doc/0006/2456601/AI-Mission-Statement-Accessible-Version.docx)):

- **Governance:** develop forums for engagement, including with industry and employee groups; be leaders in the ethical use of AI in the public service; and "ensure alignment with Commonwealth Government ethics regulations and guidance";
- **Awareness:** run education campaigns with business, industry and the community, and integrate ethical AI training into the Victorian skills system;
- **Workers:** "establish principles to guide employers in preparing for the widespread adoption of AI tools" and investigate support, including reskilling, for workers displaced by AI;
- **First Peoples:** explore First Peoples' self-determined opportunities in AI and work through the Treaty process on data sovereignty; and
- **Creators:** consider principles for ethical AI use in the creative industries aligned with Creative Australia's principles, and work with the Commonwealth to keep intellectual property laws "fit for purpose".

The "Public sector adoption" pillar commits the government to "develop appropriate review and assurance mechanisms", support public servants to "understand the risks and limitations of AI, critique AI-generated content", implement security settings to protect private and sensitive data, and "adopt best practice ethical standards for the use of AI" ([AI Mission Statement](https://djsir.vic.gov.au/__data/assets/word_doc/0006/2456601/AI-Mission-Statement-Accessible-Version.docx)). It reports that a pilot of VicGov Chat, a closed AI chat assistant built by the Department of Government Services with Cenitex, ran from April to July 2025 with 1,000 users, and that the tool is now available to Victorian Government departments and agencies ([AI Mission Statement](https://djsir.vic.gov.au/__data/assets/word_doc/0006/2456601/AI-Mission-Statement-Accessible-Version.docx)).

## Who it affects

The statement is a strategy, not a binding instrument: its key actions are framed as what the government "will" do, and it names industry, investors, universities, TAFEs, unions and the community as partners ([AI Mission Statement](https://djsir.vic.gov.au/__data/assets/word_doc/0006/2456601/AI-Mission-Statement-Accessible-Version.docx)). For businesses, its most direct effects are through investment facilitation, the data centre plan and the training programs it funds ([AI Mission Statement](https://djsir.vic.gov.au/__data/assets/word_doc/0006/2456601/AI-Mission-Statement-Accessible-Version.docx)).

For the Victorian public sector, the minimum requirements for generative AI remain those in the [Administrative Guideline for the safe and responsible use of Generative AI in the VPS](/policies/victoria-genai-guideline-vps), which "sets out minimum requirements for the use of Generative AI in the VPS" and applies to public service bodies and public entities under the Public Administration Act 2004 ([Victorian Government](https://www.vic.gov.au/administrative-guideline-safe-responsible-use-gen-ai-vps)). Among its safeguards, agency-approved generative AI tools are to be used ahead of publicly available ones, and staff "remain responsible and accountable for the accuracy and quality of their work" ([Victorian Government](https://www.vic.gov.au/administrative-guideline-safe-responsible-use-gen-ai-vps)).

## Analysis

In Policai's analysis, this is an industry strategy first. Four of its six pillars are about attracting investment, building infrastructure, growing local firms and training workers, and the money it lists goes to those ends. The ethics pillar is real, but it commits Victoria to forums, campaigns, training and principles yet to be written, not to rules.

In Policai's analysis, the most telling choice is where the statement puts the hard problems. It names privacy, intellectual property and worker protection as the areas where AI will strain existing law, then assigns them to coordination with the Commonwealth. Policai found no commitment in the statement to Victorian AI legislation or a state AI regulator. That leaves Victoria's own levers mainly in its role as a buyer and user of AI and its influence on employers.

In Policai's analysis, two promises are worth tracking because they would produce new instruments: the principles to guide employers preparing for AI adoption, and the review and assurance mechanisms for public sector AI. Neither appears in the register yet. Until they do, the generative AI guideline sets the minimum requirements for Victorian public servants, and it covers generative AI only.
