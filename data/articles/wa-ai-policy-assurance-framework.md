Western Australia governs its own use of AI through the [WA Government Artificial Intelligence Policy and Assurance Framework](/policies/wa-ai-policy-assurance-framework), first published on 5 March 2024 and last updated on 25 August 2026 ([policy and framework page](https://www.wa.gov.au/government/publications/wa-government-artificial-intelligence-policy-and-assurance-framework)). The Policy itself says it was published in March 2024 and updated in June 2025 ([AI Policy](https://www.wa.gov.au/system/files/2025-07/ai_policyv2.pdf)). Policai's guide to [how governments regulate their own use of AI](/analysis/how-governments-regulate-their-own-use-of-ai) compares it with other jurisdictions in one line. This note sets out how the scheme works.

## What it says

The Policy "establishes general principles WA Government organisations must apply to develop and use Artificial Intelligence (AI) tools ethically, safely and responsibly" ([AI Policy, Purpose](https://www.wa.gov.au/system/files/2025-07/ai_policyv2.pdf)). It adopts a definition of AI based on ISO/IEC 22989:2022, and applies to traditional AI systems and generative AI ([AI Policy, Scope and Defining artificial intelligence](https://www.wa.gov.au/system/files/2025-07/ai_policyv2.pdf)). Employees must consider whether a non-AI solution could achieve the same result, and the Policy says that when AI is used, "it must be because it is the best solution for the task" ([AI Policy, Policy requirements](https://www.wa.gov.au/system/files/2025-07/ai_policyv2.pdf)). It says AI-based functions and decisions "must always be subject to human review and intervention", and that transparency includes disclosing when an AI system is engaging with people or has been used to produce an outcome that affects them ([AI Policy, Accountability and Transparency](https://www.wa.gov.au/system/files/2025-07/ai_policyv2.pdf)).

The Policy requires each entity to nominate an AI Accountable Officer, an executive responsible for overseeing how AI is used, who approves the entity's self-assessments ([policy and framework page](https://www.wa.gov.au/government/publications/wa-government-artificial-intelligence-policy-and-assurance-framework)). The page set a deadline of close of business on 19 September 2025 for nominations ([policy and framework page](https://www.wa.gov.au/government/publications/wa-government-artificial-intelligence-policy-and-assurance-framework)). The [governance toolkit](/policies/wa-ai-governance-toolkit) lists the officer's role, including managing the risks of the entity's AI systems and tools, acting as the contact point for the Office of Digital Government, and overseeing all AI projects and tools through the AI use case register and AI tools reference list ([toolkit](https://www.wa.gov.au/organisation/department-of-the-premier-and-cabinet/office-of-digital-government/artificial-intelligence-governance-toolkit-wa-public-sector-entities)).

Entities must use the [AI Assurance Framework self-assessment](/policies/wa-ai-assurance-self-assessment) for any system or project driven by AI or automated decision making, at all phases of the project, whether the system is procured, built or otherwise sourced ([policy and framework page](https://www.wa.gov.au/government/publications/wa-government-artificial-intelligence-policy-and-assurance-framework)). The project lead completes it on an online form, which assigns an outcome and sends it to the AI Accountable Officer for approval ([policy and framework page](https://www.wa.gov.au/government/publications/wa-government-artificial-intelligence-policy-and-assurance-framework)). The template (version 2.0, June 2025) says the framework must be applied to "non-trivial systems and projects", including large language models, machine learning tools and generative AI, and lists general-purpose tools with trivial smart features, such as predictive text in Microsoft Outlook and Word, as out of scope ([self-assessment template](https://www.wa.gov.au/system/files/2025-06/selfassessmenttemplate.docx)). It asks for re-assessment at key milestones, from design and planning through to before any major change in the system or its use ([self-assessment template](https://www.wa.gov.au/system/files/2025-06/selfassessmenttemplate.docx)).

An "innovation pathway" lets entities experiment in a contained testing environment without applying the framework if no personal or sensitive government information is used, data stays on local servers or an Australian tenancy and is not given to a third party, outputs do not inform government operations, policy advice, service delivery or decisions, and no external AI software is procured or used in a free trial ([self-assessment template](https://www.wa.gov.au/system/files/2025-06/selfassessmenttemplate.docx)).

A project must go to the WA Government AI Advisory Board if its residual risk after mitigations is mid-range or higher, it is funded through the Digital Capability Fund, or its total cost exceeds $5 million ([policy and framework page](https://www.wa.gov.au/government/publications/wa-government-artificial-intelligence-policy-and-assurance-framework)). Low-risk projects need no further action ([policy and framework page](https://www.wa.gov.au/government/publications/wa-government-artificial-intelligence-policy-and-assurance-framework)). The template's risk assessment also asks whether the use case involves listed high-risk applications, such as biometric systems, employment decisions, law enforcement and access to essential services, and whether OFFICIAL Sensitive information is hosted or processed offshore ([self-assessment template, Risk assessment, steps 1 and 2](https://www.wa.gov.au/system/files/2025-06/selfassessmenttemplate.docx)). Its outcome section lists five review thresholds: mid-range or higher residual risk, a high-risk application, OFFICIAL Sensitive information hosted or processed offshore, Digital Capability Fund money, and a total cost over $5 million ([self-assessment template, Assessment outcome](https://www.wa.gov.au/system/files/2025-06/selfassessmenttemplate.docx)).

The Board may ask for a detailed risk assessment, solution architecture, requirements, the business case and a privacy impact assessment ([policy and framework page](https://www.wa.gov.au/government/publications/wa-government-artificial-intelligence-policy-and-assurance-framework)). A project can continue while under review, the Board's advice "is non-binding", and its recommendations go by letter to the entity's AI Accountable Officer and chief executive ([policy and framework page](https://www.wa.gov.au/government/publications/wa-government-artificial-intelligence-policy-and-assurance-framework)). The toolkit describes the WA Government AI Register as a collated list of AI use cases in the WA public sector based on completed self-assessments ([toolkit](https://www.wa.gov.au/organisation/department-of-the-premier-and-cabinet/office-of-digital-government/artificial-intelligence-governance-toolkit-wa-public-sector-entities)).

## Who it affects

The Policy applies to the WA public sector and to the WA Police Force, all WA government trading enterprises, the Department of the Staff of Parliament and Racing and Wagering Western Australia ([AI Policy, Scope](https://www.wa.gov.au/system/files/2025-07/ai_policyv2.pdf)). WA universities, local governments and other entities outside that scope are "encouraged to comply" ([AI Policy, Scope](https://www.wa.gov.au/system/files/2025-07/ai_policyv2.pdf)). Within an entity, the duty to complete a self-assessment falls on the lead of each AI project, and approval falls on the AI Accountable Officer ([policy and framework page](https://www.wa.gov.au/government/publications/wa-government-artificial-intelligence-policy-and-assurance-framework)). Vendors selling AI to WA agencies are not bound by the Policy, but the self-assessment asks agencies about third-party and offshore hosting of the information involved ([self-assessment template, step 2](https://www.wa.gov.au/system/files/2025-06/selfassessmenttemplate.docx)).

## Analysis

In Policai's analysis, the scheme is broad at the bottom and light at the top. Every non-trivial AI or automated decision project must be assessed, which is a wider trigger than a risk-only test, but the external check is advisory: the Board recommends, the agency decides, and a project need not pause while the Board reviews it. Accountability therefore rests with each entity's own executive, who approves the entity's own assessments.

The referral triggers are not stated the same way in both places. The policy and framework page lists three, while the template's outcome section lists five, adding high-risk applications and offshore hosting of OFFICIAL Sensitive information. An agency reading only the page could underestimate when the Board will see a project, although the online form assigns the outcome. The sources read for this note do not say whether the AI Register is published.
