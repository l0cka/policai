The [Online Safety (Basic Online Safety Expectations) Determination 2022](/policies/federal-online-safety-basic-online-safety-expectations-determination-2022) sets out what the Australian Government expects of social media services, relevant electronic services and designated internet services ([Federal Register of Legislation, F2022L00062](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2022L00062/asmade/details)). The Online Safety (Basic Online Safety Expectations) Amendment Determination 2024 inserted section 8A on generative artificial intelligence, alongside section 8B on recommender systems, in a compilation that took effect on 31 May 2024 ([F2024L00590 as made](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2024L00590/asmade/2024-05-30/text/original/pdf); [compilation](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2022L00062/2024-05-31/2024-05-31/text/original/pdf)).

## What it says

Section 8A applies "if the service uses or enables the use of generative artificial intelligence capabilities". It sets two expectations ([s 8A(1)–(2)](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2022L00062/2024-05-31/2024-05-31/text/original/pdf)):

- **Safety by design:** the provider "will take reasonable steps to consider end-user safety and incorporate safety measures in the design, implementation and maintenance of generative artificial intelligence capabilities on the service".
- **Harmful output:** the provider "will take reasonable steps to proactively minimise the extent to which generative artificial intelligence capabilities may be used to produce material or facilitate activity that is unlawful or harmful".

Its four non-limiting examples of reasonable steps are ([s 8A(3)](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2022L00062/2024-05-31/2024-05-31/text/original/pdf)):

- assessing safety risks and impacts, mitigating identified risks, and running safety review processes "throughout the design, development, deployment and post-deployment stages";
- giving end-users educational or explanatory tools about the capabilities and their risks;
- "ensuring, to the extent reasonably practicable, that training material for generative artificial intelligence capabilities and models do not contain unlawful or harmful material";
- "ensuring, to the extent reasonably practicable, that generative artificial intelligence capabilities can detect and prevent the execution of prompts that generate unlawful or harmful material".

The expectations are not directly enforceable. The Online Safety Act 2021 says a determination of basic online safety expectations "does not impose a duty that is enforceable by proceedings in a court" ([s 45(4)](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2021A00076/2026-09-12/2026-09-12/text/original/pdf)). Instead, the eSafety Commissioner can:

- require a provider, by written notice, to report on how it meets the expectations, with a civil penalty of 500 penalty units for not complying ([ss 49–50](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2021A00076/2026-09-12/2026-09-12/text/original/pdf));
- give the provider, and where appropriate publish, a statement that it has contravened or complied with the expectations ([s 48](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2021A00076/2026-09-12/2026-09-12/text/original/pdf)).

## Who it affects

Section 8A applies to any social media service, relevant electronic service or designated internet service that uses generative AI capabilities or enables their use ([s 8A](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2022L00062/2024-05-31/2024-05-31/text/original/pdf); [Online Safety Act s 45](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2021A00076/2026-09-12/2026-09-12/text/original/pdf)).

## Analysis

Section 8A works through transparency rather than direct obligation. No court can enforce it; the pressure comes from enforceable reporting notices and published statements of non-compliance.

The section 8A(3) examples read as a lifecycle checklist: risk assessment before and after release, user education, cleaner training data and prompt filtering. The phrase "to the extent reasonably practicable" on the last two examples leaves room for providers to argue about what is feasible at scale.

Section 8A now sits beside the enforceable [eSafety industry codes](/policies/esafety-genai-industry-codes), which cover generative AI services and AI companion chatbots and have applied since 9 March 2026. The Online Safety Amendment (Digital Duty of Care) Bill 2026 would repeal those codes 12 months after Royal Assent ([record](/policies/esafety-genai-industry-codes)); Policai's [note on the bill](/analysis/digital-duty-of-care-bill-2026) covers that change. This note does not assess whether the bill changes Part 4 of the Act or the expectations in this Determination.
