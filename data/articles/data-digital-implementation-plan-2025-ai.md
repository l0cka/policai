The [2025 Implementation Plan](/policies/implementation-plan-policy-responsible-use-ai-government-2025) is "the third annual update on the Government's progress against the Data and Digital Government Strategy", and it centres on four priorities over the next year: artificial intelligence, data, connected service delivery, and cyber, trust and resilience ([Data and Digital](https://www.dataanddigital.gov.au/implementation-plan/2025)). The plan's landing page was last updated on 17 November 2025 ([Data and Digital](https://www.dataanddigital.gov.au/implementation-plan/2025)). The register holds its AI chapter as active federal guidance, dated to November 2025 at month precision ([record](/policies/implementation-plan-policy-responsible-use-ai-government-2025)).

## What it says

The chapter says that "Realising the benefits of AI in Government requires a culture of innovation, accelerating the safe and responsible use of AI across all government agencies while maintaining the trust and confidence of the public" ([Data and Digital](https://www.dataanddigital.gov.au/implementation-plan/2025/artificial-intelligence)). It points to the [AI Plan for the Australian Public Service](/policies/aps-ai-plan-2025) as the path to accelerate AI adoption across the service, structured around three pillars: trust, people and tools ([Data and Digital](https://www.dataanddigital.gov.au/implementation-plan/2025/artificial-intelligence)). It also says the Government has developed a new whole-of-government Cloud policy to set clear requirements for cloud use ([Data and Digital](https://www.dataanddigital.gov.au/implementation-plan/2025/artificial-intelligence)).

On responsible use, the chapter describes the [Policy for the responsible use of AI in government](/policies/policy-responsible-use-ai-government-v2) as "the foundation for AI transformation in the Australian Public Service", released in September 2024 and mandating AI Accountable Officials and published AI Transparency Statements ([Data and Digital](https://www.dataanddigital.gov.au/implementation-plan/2025/artificial-intelligence)). It says the policy has been updated to require agencies to develop a strategic position on AI adoption and communicate it to staff, and that each in-scope use case will have a clearly assigned accountable officer and be recorded in an internal register ([Data and Digital](https://www.dataanddigital.gov.au/implementation-plan/2025/artificial-intelligence)). It says the policy mandates use of the AI Impact Assessment Tool, which evaluates the risks and impacts of AI use cases against Australia's AI Ethics Principles ([Data and Digital](https://www.dataanddigital.gov.au/implementation-plan/2025/artificial-intelligence)).

The chapter reports figures on where agencies stand ([Data and Digital](https://www.dataanddigital.gov.au/implementation-plan/2025/artificial-intelligence)):

- **Opportunities:** over 70% of agencies identified specific opportunities where AI can deliver measurable benefits to their work;
- **Monitoring:** 81% of agencies reported having measures in place to monitor the effectiveness of AI systems; and
- **Workforce:** 12.4% of Australian Public Service staff were in data and digital roles in 2025, citing the 2025 APS Census.

It describes the [AI technical standard](/policies/ai-technical-standard) as practical guidance for building, purchasing and experimenting with AI systems, written for technical specialists and business owners ([Data and Digital](https://www.dataanddigital.gov.au/implementation-plan/2025/artificial-intelligence)). It says the Government "is developing guidance to support agencies procuring AI products and services" and will keep its procurement panels under review so agencies have access to trusted AI vendors ([Data and Digital](https://www.dataanddigital.gov.au/implementation-plan/2025/artificial-intelligence)). For business, it says the [Voluntary AI Safety Standard](/policies/voluntary-ai-safety-standard) was released in late 2024 and that "In late 2025 the Government will release a new suite of industry guidance" building on it ([Data and Digital](https://www.dataanddigital.gov.au/implementation-plan/2025/artificial-intelligence)).

The rest of the chapter gives examples of agency use, including Services Australia's generative AI Digital Assistant and the National Library of Australia's transcription of 58,000 hours of oral history interviews, and describes GovAI, which the Department of Finance created so APS staff can experiment safely with AI tools ([Data and Digital](https://www.dataanddigital.gov.au/implementation-plan/2025/artificial-intelligence)).

## Who it affects

The chapter describes the Government's AI priorities for the Australian Public Service and its agencies ([Data and Digital](https://www.dataanddigital.gov.au/implementation-plan/2025/artificial-intelligence)). Policai found no requirement addressed to agencies in the chapter itself; the binding requirements it describes sit in the Policy for the responsible use of AI in government, which applies to all non-corporate Commonwealth entities, with some exceptions ([digital.gov.au](https://www.digital.gov.au/ai/ai-in-government-policy)). The Digital Transformation Agency says the current version of that policy came into effect on 15 December 2025 (v2.0), with minor updates published on 21 October 2026 (v2.1), and that the first version (v1.1) took effect on 1 September 2024 ([digital.gov.au](https://www.digital.gov.au/ai/ai-in-government-policy)). Departments and agencies must meet its mandatory requirements for accountable officials, transparency statements, a strategic approach to AI adoption, operationalising responsible AI use, use case accountability, internal use case registers, staff training and use case impact assessment ([digital.gov.au](https://www.digital.gov.au/ai/ai-in-government-policy)).

## Analysis

In Policai's analysis, the chapter is best read as a list of promises with a date attached. It was published in November 2025 for "the next year", so it can now be checked against what happened. The clearest promise was kept: the policy changes it described, a strategic position on adoption, an accountable officer for each use case and an internal register, are among the mandatory requirements of the version of the policy that took effect on 15 December 2025.

In Policai's analysis, the industry guidance line appears to have been overtaken by the time the chapter was published. The register records that the National AI Centre published the [Guidance for AI Adoption](/policies/guidance-for-ai-adoption) on 21 October 2025, superseding the Voluntary AI Safety Standard ([record](/policies/voluntary-ai-safety-standard)), yet the chapter still describes that guidance as something the Government "will release" in late 2025. The chapter does not name the Guidance for AI Adoption, so it is unclear whether the promised suite is that guidance or something further.

In Policai's analysis, one commitment remains open as far as the register shows: Policai holds no record of the AI procurement guidance the chapter says the Government is developing. The chapter's figures are also worth reading for what they are. The 70% and 81% figures describe what agencies identified and reported, not the results of independent assurance, so they show how widely AI governance measures have been adopted rather than how well they work.
